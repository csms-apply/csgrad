# Wisc MSCS

## Program Overview

This is UW–Madison's research-oriented CS master's. The career-oriented [CS PMP](../A+/Wisc%20CS%20PMP.md) is a different named option under the same degree. Their names are similar, but their emphasis differs; the named option also appears on the transcript. The Computer Sciences department is now part of the College of Computing & Artificial Intelligence.

- Fall admission only. In person and full time on the Madison campus, with no online or part-time option
- At least 30 credits. The department says it usually takes about 2 years; if you work as a TA and take only the minimum credits, it may take 2½ years
- STEM program
- Cost: the university's 2026-27 nine-month estimate for nonresident graduate students is $25,766 in tuition and fees and $50,400 in total cost of attendance, and international students pay an additional International Student Fee. The department notes that tuition can vary by program, so check the Bursar's rate tables

On funding, the department says many students admitted to the traditional MS/PhD track are offered a four-year funding guarantee through the department (the page does not separate MS from PhD). Incoming students are usually funded as TAs, which includes tuition remission and a stipend, and most assistantships are awarded at admission. A TA is typically a 50% appointment, 20 hours per week.

## Courses and Course Selection

Degree requirements in the 2026-27 Guide:

- Of the 30 credits, at least 24 must be COMP SCI courses numbered 400 or above; CS 400, 915, 699/799/899 and 702 do not count
- Of those 24, at least 15 must be core credits, meaning courses numbered 700–889 graded A–F; 838 does not count as core, and only one of the topics courses 758, 839 and 880 can count as core
- The remaining 6 credits can come from any department; the maximum is 15 credits per term, and the overall graduate GPA must be 3.00
- Both a thesis and a project are optional: CS 790 counts for at most 3 credits with a project report and at most 6 credits with a master's thesis
- Graduate credits from other institutions cannot be transferred in

700-level courses include 736 Advanced Operating Systems, 739 Distributed Systems, 744 Big Data Systems, 752 Advanced Computer Architecture I, 760 Machine Learning, 764 Topics in Database Management Systems and 782 Advanced Computer Security and Privacy. Check each term's schedule for what is actually offered.

You are not assigned an advisor when you start and have to find one yourself; most students have one by their second year. Until then, the Graduate Advising Committee (GAC) advises on course selection.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

The department encourages internships but does not require them. Past internship employers listed on its page include PayPal, Amazon, Google, Fetch, Roku and Qualcomm. You can earn credit for an internship through CS 702: register for 2 credits if the internship totals 360 hours or more in the term, or 1 credit for 150–359 hours.

CPT for international students works differently by term:

- Summer: after you have the offer and employment letter, ask a faculty member (usually your advisor) for permission to enroll in 702, then submit the department's CPT form and a Terra Dotta verification request
- Fall / spring: first email the GAC for departmental approval, your advisor has to reply confirming support, and you cannot hold a CS TA position that term. If approved by July 15 for fall or November 13 for spring, you can still withdraw from a TA position you already accepted; after August 1 for fall or December 1 for spring, CPT will not be approved if you have accepted a TA obligation

So a term-time internship basically requires having an advisor first, and for that term it is an either-or choice with a CS TA position.

## Jobs and Location

The program is on the main Madison campus. The Careers section of the department's website links to Student Career Resources, the CAI Career Fair and the Industrial Affiliates Program. It is a STEM program, so graduates can apply for STEM OPT under the applicable rules.

## Application Requirements

- Fall only, with a December 15 deadline; for Fall 2027 that means December 15, 2026. Decisions are emailed by late February, admitted students have until April 15 to respond, and there is no waitlist
- MS and PhD applicants are reviewed in one pool, with no different criteria for MS versus PhD, and the department explicitly says not to apply to both. An MS student who later wants the PhD does not need to reapply but must pass the PhD Qualifying Process
- GRE not required; scores may be reviewed if submitted
- English follows the Graduate School requirements: TOEFL 92 (tests taken before January 21, 2026) or 5.0 on the new format (tests on or after January 21, 2026), IELTS 7.0, Duolingo 125, and scores must be less than two years old on the first day of the enrollment term
- Materials: SOP, CV, 3 recommendation letters and unofficial transcripts. An SOP of 1 to 1½ pages is enough; use at most one work reference, with the other two academic
- Background: a CS undergraduate degree is not required, but you need documented coursework or skills, and the department strongly recommends college-level math such as calculus plus programming courses
- Application fee $75 (plus a $6 processing fee for international applicants), and one fee covers up to 3 programs in the same entry term

## Admissions Results (csgrad DP)

csgrad has 13 Wisc MSCS data points, all from Fall 2023 and Fall 2024, with none yet from Fall 2025 or later:

- Fall 2023: 2 admits, 5 rejects
- Fall 2024: 2 admits, 4 rejects

Of the 4 admits, 2 had US undergraduate CS degrees (GPA 3.80–3.91) and 2 were mainland China 985 CS undergraduates (3.9+ on a 4.0 scale or 94+ on a 100-point scale). The 3 who submitted GRE scored 322–334 in total, and the English scores reported were TOEFL 107 and IELTS 7. All 3 entries with notes said there was no interview. The 2 Fall 2023 admits were marked as funded.

All 9 rejects were mainland China or Hong Kong undergraduates, from 985, "Huawu" top-five, 211, UCAS/ShanghaiTech/SUSTech and Hong Kong schools, majoring in CS, software engineering, information security and math. GPAs were 3.80–3.92 on a 4.0 scale or 86–94 on a 100-point scale (plus one on a 4.3 scale), and 4 of them ranked in the top 1%–3%. GRE ranged from 322 to 330 and TOEFL from 102 to 114, and most had 1 to 4 research experiences.

The sample is small, but it shows that mainland applicants with very high GPAs and rankings are rejected fairly often.
