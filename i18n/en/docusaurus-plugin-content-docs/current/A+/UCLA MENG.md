# UCLA MEng
## Program Experience
2026 update: starting with Fall 2026, it has been confirmed that UCLA MEng can be extended and that students can do a co-op.

UCLA has a great location, pleasant weather, convenient living and a good ranking. The campus is in Westwood on the west side of Los Angeles, not far from LAX, with Santa Monica Beach, Hollywood and Beverly Hills nearby. There are plenty of restaurants and supermarkets around campus, and a 99 Ranch Market opened recently. I haven't been yet, but friends say it's good. For housing, you can apply for the graduate housing at Weyburn Terrace, which costs around \$1,200-1,500, with food and groceries close by. If you don't get on-campus housing, Westwood, Sawtelle, Century City and Culver City are all options for renting, not far from campus and with a reasonable commute.

The program is a Master of Engineering (MEng) in the Samueli School of Engineering, meant to compete with UC Berkeley's MEng. It is a one-year program of three quarters plus one summer session, but you can extend by one quarter and graduate in December, which leaves time for a summer internship and for waiting on a return offer. If you land a full-time job before graduating, you can also finish in one year and start earning sooner.

Fall 2023 admitted about 150 students, mostly in the Data Science and AI tracks. Most students are Chinese, with many from mainland Chinese undergraduate programs and many with a US undergraduate degree.

## Cost

The MEng is a self-funded professional master's program at a public university. Unlike the flat tuition of undergraduate and MS programs, MEng tuition is charged per unit: \$1,470 per unit, and a course is usually 4 units. Under normal circumstances, total tuition is \$52,920.

## Curriculum

### Graduation Requirements

To graduate, MEng students must complete the following:

- 2 technical core courses

- 3 technical elective courses

- 3 professional development courses

- 1 capstone project

(For the full list, see the [UCLA MEng program requirements](https://grad.ucla.edu/programs/school-of-engineering-and-applied-science/engineering-master-of-engineering/).)

For the two main tracks, Data Science and AI, the courses are mostly data science and machine learning, some of them fairly theoretical, with no systems or programming courses. If you want to move into SDE, this program may not be a good fit, since taking foundational CS courses outside the list requires the director's approval.

The professional development courses focus on soft skills such as communication, project management and finance, and some cover data analytics and decision-making. They don't help much with job hunting directly, but they can improve how you present yourself, and many of them are said to be graded leniently, so they are fairly easy to get through.

There is no thesis requirement, but you need to do a capstone project in the summer.

### Standard Course Plan

The program runs Fall + Winter + Spring + Summer, 12 months in total. You need to complete eight courses across Fall, Winter and Spring, and finish the capstone project in the summer. Usually you take 2-3 courses per quarter.

Note that core courses are usually not offered in Spring.

### Course Substitution

If the elective list doesn't have a course you want, you can use a one-time petition during the year. With the program director's approval, you can replace one of your three electives, for example with an undergraduate OS or Architecture course.

### Capstone Project

For Fall 2026, the capstone can be completed in several ways:

- Option 1: Group Capstone Project (Traditional Pathway)

  - Participation in an industry or faculty-sponsored capstone project during the summer.

- Option 2A: Industry Internship and Technical Report

  - Completion of a 10–12 week (minimum 400 hours) full-time technical internship during the summer.

- Option 2B: Two-Quarter Industry Internship (Summer and Fall)

  - Completion of a 20–24-week full-time technical internship during Summer and Fall. Meanwhile keeping enrollment in a special topic 4-unit course in Fall.

The Traditional Pathway is usually done with a partner company that provides the topic. It can be done remotely, alongside an internship, and ends with a final presentation. However, the partner companies are mostly local businesses with few big tech firms, so building big tech connections through the capstone is probably not realistic.

### Non-Standard Course Plan: Extending

With a weak job market, more students want to extend so they get one more fall recruiting window or more time to prepare for further study.

UCLA MEng offers two ways to push graduation to December of the year after you enroll (making the program about 15 months to 1.5 years long).

- Capstone Project Option 2B:  
  As described above, if you secure an internship for the following summer during fall or spring recruiting, you can use capstone Option 2B and an internship of up to 24 weeks to extend your full-time enrollment status to December of the following year.

- Incomplete Degree Requirement  
  As confirmed by the Fall 2026 academic advisor, if you haven't completed the "3 professional development courses" requirement by the following summer, you can, with a simple approval, take the remaining professional development course in the following Fall quarter.  
  Because that remaining course is a required course being made up under non-standard circumstances, a special exception applies to that Fall quarter: registering for just 4 units counts as full-time enrollment (normal full-time enrollment requires 8 units per quarter).
  This means you can push back your graduation date without paying for extra courses, which is a very good deal.  

  For example, the following schedule is allowed:  
  - 26Fall: 2 elective courses + 1 petitioned undergrad OS course  
  - 26Winter: 2 core courses  
  - 27Spring: 2 professional development courses  
  - 27Summer: Capstone Project  
  - 27Fall: 1 professional development course

## Teaching and Research

UCLA's CS teaching and research resources are weaker than the top four (the "T0" schools) and some other strong CS schools, and slightly below T1 schools such as GT, UCSD, UW, UIUC and UT. MEng research training is also not on par with a traditional research MS. None of that stops UCLA MEng from increasingly becoming the best-value engineering master's program in the US. Because the UCLA name carries a lot of weight overall (although I did once have a coworker at a big tech company who knew USC but not UCLA), the MEng has big advantages in terms of admission bar and profile building.

From my experience, compared with the UK, West Coast universities do have deeper ties to industry. For example, MEng students can take a seminar run by an AWS VP, with a Principal Applied Scientist from his team as lecturer. Many of the questions discussed in class come from real industry problems, and you get to hear a lot of different perspectives.

## Job Search Data Points

For job hunting, the school career fair is mediocre, and most of the companies there are ones you've never heard of. The program's career counselor regularly sends out job postings, but the quality isn't great; you're better off browsing LinkedIn or Glassdoor yourself. Overall, you have to rely on yourself for job hunting, and the program doesn't help much.

CPT/OPT support is decent. The program is STEM-designated, so OPT is available for 3 years. CPT applications require completing one academic year (Fall, Winter, Spring).

## Who This Program Suits

If you already have work experience and are mainly coming for the degree or a work visa, and don't care much about course content, this program is a reasonable fit. LA has great weather, life isn't very stressful, and the course workload isn't heavy, so you can study and job hunt at the same time, and the overall experience is pretty good. But if you want to grow through the program's courses and resources, or rely on its career support to get into big tech, you may want to think again, since the program's own resources are fairly limited.
