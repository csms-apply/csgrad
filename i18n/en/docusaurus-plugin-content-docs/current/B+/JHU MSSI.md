# JHU MSSI

## Program Overview

The Master of Science in Security Informatics (MSSI) at Johns Hopkins sits under the Information Security Institute (ISI) of the Whiting School of Engineering in the catalogue and is run together with the Department of Computer Science; the program page and the admissions page both live on the CS department's website. It is a full-time day program on the Homewood campus in Baltimore.

- Length: 10 courses (3 or more credits each) plus a capstone. The program page gives an average of 3 full-time semesters, and the catalogue requires at least 2 full-time resident semesters
- Start terms: fall and spring
- Cost: 2026-27 tuition for full-time Whiting graduate students is $68,670 per year, or about $103,005 for 3 semesters. The graduate budget on the same university page puts food, housing, books, transportation, personal expenses and health insurance at $27,929 per year, plus a $500 matriculation fee. The catalogue says MSSI students are not normally eligible for tuition waivers
- Funding: the ISI Fellowship is merit-based and goes to selected admits with no separate application. It is paid in two halves against first- and second-semester tuition, and the second half requires a B average and continuous full-time status. The page does not give an amount. The DoW Cyber Service Academy and NSF CyberAI SFS are only open to U.S. citizens or permanent residents
- STEM: neither the program page nor the catalogue lists a CIP code or STEM designation; if it matters to you, email JHUISI-MSSI@jhu.edu
- Dual degrees: a two-year dual master's with the CS department's MSECS, with up to 2 courses counting toward both degrees and admission decided case by case; there is also a dual degree with Applied Mathematics & Statistics

Both tracks include policy or management alongside technical security. Compare the course mix with your goals in research, engineering or policy.

## Courses and Course Selection

Courses fall into four areas, Technology, Policy, Management and Health, and each area is further split into Core, Elective and Foundational. There are two tracks:

- Technology & Research: 5 technology courses (at least 4 Core, including at least 1 Core Cryptography course), 3 Core policy/management/health courses (at least 1 policy and 1 management), and 2 more technology courses
- Policy & Management: 3 technology courses (at least 2 Core, including 1 cryptography course), 5 policy/health/management courses (at least 1 from each of the three, plus at least 1 Foundational Management course), and 2 more courses

So even the technology track includes 3 policy and management courses. Other rules: courses must be 600-level or above; the courses counted toward the degree need a GPA of 3.0 or higher; at most 2 courses below B- can count, and nothing below C- counts; at most 2 independent study courses and at most 2 transfer courses. You also have to pass Graduate Academic Ethics (EN.500.603) and the online Responsible Conduct of Research (AS.360.624).

The capstone is a team project with both a technical and a non-technical component. It can be sponsored by government or industry or tied to faculty research, and it ends with a report and a presentation.

Core technology courses in the 2026-27 catalogue include Network Security, Software Vulnerability Analysis, Ethical Hacking, Computer Forensics, Web Security and Cloud Computing Security; on the cryptography side there are Introduction to Cryptography, Modern Cryptography and Practical Cryptographic Systems.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

- On-campus work: the catalogue says MSSI students can work on campus up to 19.99 hours per week; TA and RA positions are for PhD students only, but master's students can work as course assistants
- APL summer internship: the Hopkins-APL Master's Scholars program mentioned on the program page is a paid summer internship at APL in Laurel, Maryland. The summer 2027 round requires U.S. citizenship and the ability to obtain a security clearance
- CPT: JHU's OIS CPT page (checked in October 2026) says that after new SEVP guidance in August, JHU only authorizes new CPT for internships or practicums required for degree completion, and its eligibility list requires a degree program with an internship, practicum or co-op required of all students. The MSSI degree requirements do not include one. You also need to finish one academic year before starting CPT, and JHU summers do not count as a semester. OIS suggests pre-completion OPT for internships that are not required. The policy is still changing, so go by OIS's latest guidance
- Neither the program page nor the catalogue mentions co-op

## Jobs and Location

The school is in Baltimore, not far from Washington, D.C. The program page highlights the NSA, the Pentagon, federal agencies and the defense industry nearby, but the government-linked opportunities on the page (the APL internship, CSA and SFS) all require U.S. citizenship or permanent residency. Recent alumni employers listed on the program page include TSMC, Huawei, Waymo, Cognizant, ByteDance, Millennium Management and Fidelity Investments. No employment rate is published.

International students should check eligibility carefully: the listed APL internship, CSA and SFS opportunities have citizenship or permanent residency restrictions. The program does not publish an employment rate.

## Application Requirements

- Deadline: March 15, 2027, 11:59 p.m. EST for Fall 2027, and November 15, 2026 for Spring 2027. The banner at the top of the CS department website says to apply by February 15, which does not match the MSSI admissions page, so the earlier date is the safer one. To be considered for the ISI Fellowship, submit your application by the deadline
- No application fee. The GRE is not required and is optional to submit
- English: international applicants submit TOEFL or IELTS. The page lists preferred scores of TOEFL 100 and IELTS 7.0, marked as general reference only; Duolingo is not mentioned
- Materials: 2 recommendation letters, a statement of purpose, transcripts and a resume/CV
- Background: you need enough IT preparation. The page names computer architecture/networking, programming, data structures and discrete math, which can be shown through coursework and in some cases through work experience or professional certifications

## Admissions Data (csgrad DP)

csgrad has 4 data points for JHU MSSI, all admits:

- Fall 2023: 1 admit, marked as funded with no amount given
- Fall 2025: 3 admits, all notified in late March 2025
- No rejects or waitlists recorded, and no data for Fall 2024 or Fall 2026

All 4 had bachelor's degrees from mainland China, one each from a 985 university, a 211 university, a non-985/211 university and a Sino-foreign joint university; 3 majored in information security and 1 in CS. GPAs were 3.85–3.88 on a 4-point scale (2 people) or 87–91.67 out of 100 (2 people); the two who gave a class rank were about top 10% and top 20%. English scores were TOEFL 99–103 (3 people) or IELTS 7.5 (1 person). The 2 who submitted the GRE scored 320 and 324, both with Q170. Three reported research experience in China, 1 reported an internship in China, and nobody listed a publication.

With only 4 data points and no rejects, treat this as a rough picture only.
