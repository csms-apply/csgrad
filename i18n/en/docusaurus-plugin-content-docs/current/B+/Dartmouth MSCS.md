# Dartmouth MSCS

## Program Overview

This is the Master of Science in Computer Science from Dartmouth's Department of Computer Science, administered by the Guarini School of Graduate and Advanced Studies, on the campus in Hanover, New Hampshire. The department offers two MS programs: the standard MSCS, with a coursework track and a research thesis track, and the MS in CS with Concentration in Digital Arts (CS/DA). Each has a 4+1 version only for current Dartmouth undergraduates, which does not apply to outside applicants.

- Length: the department describes the standard MSCS as a 2-year (7-term) program. Dartmouth runs on a quarter system with fall, winter, spring and summer terms; the ORC sets a maximum of seven consecutive terms, not counting summers when you are not enrolled
- Start term: the Guarini FAQ says most programs admit for fall only
- Cost: on Guarini's 2026-27 rate table, tuition is $23,899 per term, or $71,697 for 3 terms. With housing, food, books, transportation and fees added, the 3-term (9-month) budget for international students comes to $109,914.50, with the student health plan at $5,216 on top
- Funding: Guarini's funding package (tuition, health insurance and a stipend) covers PhD students and a few named programs, and the CS MS is not one of them; the page says other programs provide limited financial assistance on terms each program sets
- STEM: neither the department nor Guarini lists a CIP code or STEM designation; if it matters to you, ask ms@cs.dartmouth.edu

Students interested in research should note that new MSCS students enter the coursework track first and apply to transfer to the thesis track. Digital Arts requires a thesis and is better suited to an existing interest in visual computing or digital arts research.

## Courses and Course Selection

The following is from the 2026-27 ORC. All graduate students take COSC 700 (research ethics) in their first year.

- Coursework track: at least 13 CS courses taken for graduate credit, with at least 5 numbered 130 or higher and at least 1 advanced topics course (149/249, 169/269, 189/289), plus an essay approved by the program director explaining how the courses form a coherent whole. Up to 2 research credit courses (297–299) can count, only with a P or HP and the director's approval
- Thesis track: at least 9 courses plus at least 5 research credits, a thesis and a public defense. Everyone starts in the coursework track and petitions to switch by the end of the third term
- Department rule: some MS students need to take CS 10, and anyone who gets below a B in it is asked to leave the program immediately

The Digital Arts concentration always requires a thesis and totals 18 credits: two of COSC 30/31/50/70 as prerequisites (equivalents taken before enrolling also count), at least 5 technical courses numbered 131–189 or 231–289 (including 1 advanced topics course), at least 2 digital arts courses (taking one outside CS is encouraged, and all must be finished by the end of the fifth term), and at least 6 research credits. Research topics fall in visual computing, such as graphics, HCI, digital fabrication, computer vision and VR/AR.

Graduate courses in the ORC catalog include COSC 257 Compilers, 258 Operating Systems, 274 Machine Learning and Statistical Data Analysis, 277 Computer Graphics, 278 Deep Learning and 287 Rendering Algorithms, with topics courses such as 189.34 AI Agents. Check each term's schedule for what is actually offered.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

- No graduate-credit courses are offered in summer, and the department says most MS students are away for the summer term
- For every 3 graduate credits counting toward the degree, you may take 1 term away from campus (internship / CPT or vacation). The credits must be earned in advance; summer is the exception, where you may leave first and have credits earned afterward count toward that summer
- The internship course is COSC 295 (Experiential Learning in Graduate Computer Science). It is pass/fail, does not count toward the degree's course total, needs the program director's approval and is usually taken in summer. The department signs off only if you can show the internship adds to your education
- The department page also says international students can take at most one year of CPT in total and at most one vacation term per academic year. Check visa details with OVIS, Dartmouth's visa office

## Jobs and Location

Hanover is in the Upper Valley. Guarini's transportation page says the Dartmouth Coach runs daily direct buses to Boston South Station and Logan Airport, with direct service to New York City as well; Lebanon Municipal Airport has daily flights to Boston and White Plains, New York; and Manchester Airport is about an hour south of Hanover.

Hanover is a small college town some distance from large cities. Consider whether the setting suits you and how you would arrange travel for interviews and other trips.

## Application Requirements

- Deadline: Guarini's CS page lists December 15 with no year; for the Fall 2027 cycle that is December 15, 2026. Guarini says decisions go out between January and April depending on the program, and applying early does not get you an early decision
- Application fee $150. The GRE is not accepted
- English: non-US citizens submit TOEFL, IELTS or Duolingo, unless they hold or are earning a bachelor's or higher degree from a US institution or from a school where English is the sole language of instruction. Guarini recommends TOEFL 100+, IELTS 7+ and Duolingo 135+, as recommended ranges rather than hard cutoffs
- 3 recommendation letters required, up to 4 accepted
- A personal statement of up to 1,000 words covering three things: your path here, what excites you intellectually and why Dartmouth. Also a CV and unofficial transcripts
- Supplement: a 100-line sample of code you wrote, with a paragraph on why you chose it; and a CS paper you wrote (for a conference, a class or out of personal interest; for multi-author work, choose the part that is mainly yours and explain your contribution), 2 pages for MS CS and optional for CS/DA
- For CS/DA, choose Digital Arts in the Area of Interest box; for the standard MSCS, choose Not Applicable. CS/DA applicants also share a portfolio link, need the equivalent of Dartmouth's COSC 1 and COSC 10 with a B+ or better, and should have majored or minored in a foundational area such as CS, digital arts, engineering, design, architecture, physics or math

## Admissions Data (csgrad DP)

csgrad has 7 data points for Dartmouth MSCS and 1 for CS/DA:

- MSCS Fall 2023: 1 admit, 1 waitlist
- MSCS Fall 2024: 2 admits, 1 reject
- MSCS Fall 2026: 2 admits
- CS/DA Fall 2025: 1 admit
- No MSCS data for Fall 2025 yet

All 6 admits were marked as funded, and 4 noted a scholarship percentage: one 50% and three 75%. The admits with dates were all notified between mid-February and mid-March.

Of the 5 MSCS admits, 4 had bachelor's degrees from mainland China (one each from a "Huawu" top-five university, a 985 university, a 211 university and a Sino-foreign joint institute) and 1 from Canada, in CS, software engineering and AI. Four had GPAs of 87–92 out of 100; the other had a 3.33 on a 4-point scale (roughly top 40%). The 3 who took the GRE scored 323–327, and English scores were TOEFL 105 or IELTS 7. Four listed research experience, 2 of them abroad, and 2 had first-author papers.

The reject was a 985 CS applicant with 4.33 on a 5-point scale, GRE 325, TOEFL 105, research in China and abroad, and two internships. The waitlisted applicant was a 985 math/statistics major with about 87 out of 100, GRE 322, IELTS 8 and three research experiences. The CS/DA admit came from an interdisciplinary program at a "Huawu" top-five university with a design background, 3.78 on a 4-point scale, TOEFL 106 and one research experience.

All the GRE scores come from 2023 and 2024 applicants; the two Fall 2026 data points have no GRE, consistent with the current no-GRE policy. The sample is very small, so treat it as a rough picture only.
