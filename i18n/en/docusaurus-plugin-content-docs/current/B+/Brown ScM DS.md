# Brown ScM DS

## Program Overview

Brown's Master's in Data Science awards a Master of Science (Sc.M.). It is run by the Data Science Institute (DSI) together with the Division of Applied Mathematics and the Department of Computer Science. It is taught entirely in person on the campus in Providence, Rhode Island.

- Length: fall starters can finish in 12, 16, 21 or 24 months; spring starters can choose 16, 21 or 24 months, with no 12-month option
- Start terms: most students start in fall (early September); there is also a spring intake (late January)
- Size: the FAQ says the program usually receives 700–800 applications a year and aims for a cohort of 50–60 students
- Cost: Brown's 2026-27 master's tuition table lists Data Science ScM at $10,196 per course, 9 courses, $91,764 in total, with the course load shown as 2+2+2+2+1 (the last course in summer). DSI's apply page says this is the tuition, excluding fees, for Fall 2026 entrants finishing in 12 months; tuition is charged per course and usually rises 4–5% each academic year, so taking longer costs slightly more in total. 2026-27 also has a $1,296 annual health services fee and a $5,283 annual student health insurance fee
- STEM: the FAQ says it counts as a STEM program for OPT extension purposes
- Scholarships: there are 100%, 50% and 25% tuition scholarships, open only to U.S. citizens and permanent residents

Before choosing a timeline, consider the tradeoff: a 12-month schedule compresses study but leaves less room for internships and job searching. The 16–24 month options give you more time, though a longer stay also means more living expenses and exposure to tuition increases.

## Courses and Course Selection

The degree is 9 courses: 4 core courses (including the capstone), 4 restricted electives and 1 unrestricted elective.

Core courses:

- DATA 1030 Hands-on Data Science, usually offered in fall, taught in Python
- DATA 1050 Data Engineering, usually offered in fall, covering databases, SQL, Hadoop and cloud computing
- DATA 2010 Math in Machine Learning
- DATA 2050 Data Practicum, usually offered in spring and summer, with DATA 1030 and 1050 as prerequisites. Students work in teams on a real data science problem. The curriculum page says the partner can be Brown faculty or an external company, while the practicum page says the project must be with an external company; students can find their own project or pick one the program provides

Exceptionally well-prepared students can replace some core courses with restricted electives, approved case by case by the DGS and the instructors; DATA 2050 cannot be replaced.

At least 1 of the 4 restricted electives must come from the Responsible Data Science category; the full list is in the Program Bulletin. The curriculum page suggests course sets by focus area: Data Analyst, General Data Scientist, Machine Learning Engineer, Machine Learning Researcher, Public Good Data Scientist, Computational Biology/Genomics, and Healthcare Analytics/Public Health. DATA 2080, which appears in some of these sets, will not be offered until Fall 2027.

The unrestricted elective can be any graduate course (four-digit number not starting with 0), taken for a letter grade; for courses outside DSI, CS and Applied Math, check with the DGS first. The FAQ says coursework is mainly in Python and R, and transfer credits are not accepted.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

DSI's pages do not cover CPT specifically. The curriculum page mentions internships only under the Machine Learning Engineer area: students who can substitute two core courses are advised to take APCOMP 215 and DATA 2980, the latter to cover an internship. The FAQ says there is no part-time option and no way to do the program while working full time.

Brown ISSS posted a CPT notice on September 2, 2026: after SEVP issued its interpretation of the F-1 CPT rules on August 24, ISSS will, until further notice, only approve CPT where the practical training is integral to the curriculum and required of every student in the program to graduate. The DS master's curriculum page does not make an internship required for everyone, so if you plan a summer internship during the program, check with ISSS and the program before you enroll.

## Jobs and Location

The school is in Providence, Rhode Island. DSI's career outcomes page is based on alumni LinkedIn profiles, as of August 2024:

- Since the first cohort enrolled in 2017, 235 students have finished the program; 181 have employment information, and about 75% work in finance, tech or health, or went on to a PhD
- 2021 cohort, 46 graduates (most in May 2023): 38 employed, 3 looking, 5 unknown
- 2022 cohort, 40 graduates (most in May 2024): two to three months after graduation, 20 employed, 8 looking, 12 unknown

Employers the page marks as having multiple alumni: Amazon, ByteDance/TikTok, Capital One, Citizens Bank, LinkedIn, Meta, Oracle, Visa and Zest AI.

The outcomes figures have unknown destinations in each cohort. They are not an employment rate for all graduates and do not describe the current hiring market.

## Application Requirements

- Deadlines: February 1 for the fall intake, with decisions in late March; for the spring intake, October 1 for international students and November 1 for domestic students, with rolling admissions. Applications open July 15. The site gives no year; by this schedule, Fall 2027 means February 1, 2027
- GRE: not required, and submitted scores are not evaluated
- English: applicants whose native language is not English and who do not hold a degree from an English-taught institution must submit TOEFL or IELTS. The site says it prefers TOEFL iBT above 105, with a minimum of 5.5 on the new TOEFL scale, and a minimum IELTS of 7.5
- 3 recommendation letters; if you have relevant work, internship or RA/TA experience, letters from supervisors are recommended, and instructor letters only help if you worked with the instructor regularly
- Essays: no personal statement. Instead there are two short-answer questions (up to 250 words each), one on a skill or experience that sets you apart and one on your post-graduation goals and how they fit Brown and DSI, plus a short video about an internship, research or course project you enjoyed. A CV is required; a writing sample is optional
- Transcripts: unofficial transcripts to apply, official ones after admission and before classes start; non-U.S. degrees need an evaluation from WES, ECE or The Evaluation Company
- Recommended preparation: one semester each of calculus, linear algebra and calculus-based probability and statistics, an intro programming course, and familiarity with Python or R. Brown requires a minimum GPA of 3.0/4.0, with exceptions for unusual circumstances
- Interviews: the site only says scholarship candidates may be invited to a Zoom interview
