# UBC MSCS

## Program Overview

The official degree is the Master of Science in Computer Science (MSc), offered by the Department of Computer Science in the Faculty of Science at UBC Vancouver, and taught in person. It is a research-based master's: the department describes it as a two-year program that includes about 16 months of full-time research. You can start in September or January. According to the graduate school, students who graduated in 2021–2024 took 2.3 years on average.

The department says the MSc is fully funded. The graduate school page lists the guaranteed minimum support: CAD $32,171 per year for two years on the thesis track, and $22,264 per year for 20 months on the essay track. It comes as a mix of awards, TA and RA positions, and requires full-time registration and satisfactory progress. First-year students also receive the Faculty of Science Master's Tuition Award, currently $5,515.71 per year, for the first year only.

Tuition, from the graduate school program page (checked October 2026; it is labeled first-year tuition but does not name an academic year): CAD $10,081.65 per year for international students and $5,738.52 for domestic students, usually rising 2%–5% a year. Student fees are about $1,203.20 per year. Eligible international students also receive an International Tuition Award of $3,200 per year.

Canada has no equivalent of the US STEM designation. UBC's international student guide says that since February 15, 2024, graduates of master's programs of 8 months or longer can get a 3-year PGWP, master's degrees are exempt from the field-of-study requirement, and PGWP applicants need English at CLB 7.

## Courses and Course Selection

The department's MSc requirements offer three ways to complete the degree:

- 18 course credits + a 12-credit thesis (CPSC 549B): a common plan is three graduate courses per term in the first year, six in total
- 24 course credits + a 6-credit thesis: the department does not encourage it, because it usually takes far more than half the work of the 12-credit thesis
- 27 course credits + a 3-credit essay (CPSC 589): the courses must cover 5 research areas, with at least 21 credits in computer science; the essay is a literature survey of one area and does not need new results. The department calls this a faster route to the MSc for students planning a PhD

At most 6 credits of 300–400-level undergraduate courses count. Both the thesis and the essay must be approved by a committee of at least two faculty members, and you give a department seminar unless you have already presented the work at a conference.

Each new student is assigned a temporary advisor by the department, who does not necessarily become the supervisor. Students must find a thesis or essay supervisor by March 15 of the first year. Without one by July 1 of year one, funding stops after August 31 and resumes once you find one; without one by January 1 of year two, you are withdrawn from the program. January starters have only about two and a half months, so the department suggests contacting faculty before you arrive.

## Internships / Co-op

With a signed consent form from their supervisor, MSc students can apply to the department's co-op program for up to two work terms. Work terms must be paid and full-time, usually start 6–7 months after you apply, and you must be willing to take a placement outside the Lower Mainland.

During a co-op term you stay registered full-time and pay tuition, and your supervisor does not pay an RA stipend. A co-op term cannot be your last term, and it does not automatically extend your program deadlines. The alternative is an internship on a Professional Leave of Absence, which does extend your deadlines, but you are not a full-time student during it, and international students cannot use their study permit to work full-time.

During academic sessions, international students with a valid study permit can work on campus without an hour limit and off campus up to 24 hours a week.

## Jobs and Location

The school is in Vancouver. The graduate school page only says that the department has many industry contacts and that faculty and students work with industry through internships, consulting and startups; there is no employment data. To stay and work in Canada after graduating, you would use the PGWP described above.

The csgrad DP database has no job-search records for this program yet.

## Application Requirements

For Fall 2027 (the same round also covers January 2028 starts), based on the official pages in October 2026:

- Deadline: December 15, 2026, for both international and Canadian applicants; the application opens September 15. Transcripts are due by December 16, and references by January 15, 2027
- Grades: the graduate school minimum is usually a B+ average (76% at UBC); the program may set higher requirements, and admission is competitive
- GRE: not required
- English (required for applicants whose undergraduate university outside Canada does not teach primarily in English; scores must be from the last 24 months): TOEFL taken before January 21, 2026 (120-point scale): 100, with Reading 22, Listening 22, Writing 21 and Speaking 21; TOEFL taken on or after that date (6-point scale): overall 5.0, with Reading 4.5, Listening 5.0, Writing 4.5 and Speaking 4.0. IELTS Academic 7.0 with no band below 6.5; Duolingo 130
- References: 3
- Supervisors: you name the faculty you want to work with in the application; the graduate school page says contacting faculty beforehand is not required
- International application fee: CAD $168.25

## Admission Results (csgrad DP)

csgrad has only 4 data points for UBC MSCS, all for fall intake:

| Intake | Result |
|---|---|
| Fall 2023 | 1 admit (funded, enrolled) |
| Fall 2024 | 1 silent rejection |
| Fall 2025 | 1 withdrawn |
| Fall 2026 | 1 reject |

All four applicants studied at mainland Chinese universities: 2 from non-985/211 schools, 1 from a 985 school and 1 from a Sino-foreign joint institute, majoring in CS, software engineering or AI. GPAs were 88–93 on a 100-point scale, and reported ranks ranged from the top 1% to the top 30%. Two submitted TOEFL (105 and 106) and one IELTS 7.0; the two who submitted GRE scored 323 and 326, with Quant 168–170. All four had research experience.

The only admit already had several papers (mostly not as first author) and an industry research internship when applying, and the notes say it required contacting professors in advance, involved an interview, and the professor made the decision. The silent rejection notes that the applicant did not contact any professors and never heard back. The reject had a first-author paper at a top venue and an IELTS speaking score of 6.0, below the program's 6.5 minimum.

The sample is far too small to say much. One thing stands out: the official page says contacting faculty is not required, but the only admit in the DP came after reaching out to a professor, an interview and a professor's decision.
