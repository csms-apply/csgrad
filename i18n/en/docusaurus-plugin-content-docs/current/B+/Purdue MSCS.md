# Purdue MSCS

## Program Overview

This is the master's in Purdue's Department of Computer Science. The Graduate School's program page lists the degree as a Master of Science (MS), with thesis and non-thesis options. The MS and the PhD share the same application process and deadlines.

- Campus: West Lafayette, Indiana
- Credits: the non-thesis option is 10 three-credit courses; the thesis option is 8 courses plus at least 6 credits of thesis research (CS 69800) and an oral defense
- Length: the department says it usually takes three or four semesters, and finishing within twelve months is sometimes possible. The practical maximum is 4 courses per semester and 2 in summer
- Entry: Fall and Spring; no Summer entry
- STEM: neither the department nor the Graduate School page gives a CIP code. ISS says STEM OPT eligibility depends on whether the CIP code after your major on page 1 of the I-20 is on the DHS STEM list, and that code cannot be changed later
- Cost: in the Bursar's 2026-27 graduate tuition table, the Computer Science & Engineering and Agricultural & Biological Engineering rate adds $562 per semester on top of standard tuition. International students pay $15,159 per semester, or $30,318 per academic year; with the university's estimated on-campus housing and food, books, transportation and miscellaneous costs, the yearly total is $50,922
- Funding: admitted applicants are automatically considered for fellowships and teaching assistantships, students with doctoral potential get priority, and admission without support is possible. The department says that in Fall 2023 all incoming PhD students and "selected" MS students were supported. TAs must first be certified in oral English (TOEFL, IELTS or OEPT)

## Courses and Course Selection

Both options require 3 core courses, one from each group:

- Systems I: CS 50200 Compiling and Programming Systems or CS 56500 Programming Languages
- Systems II: CS 50300 Operating Systems or CS 53600 Data Communication and Computer Networks
- Algorithms: CS 58000 Algorithm Design, Analysis, and Implementation or CS 58800 Randomized Algorithms

You then take 4 courses from the department's areas-and-courses table, covering at least two areas beyond the core ones. The AI area includes Statistical Machine Learning, Foundations of Deep Learning, NLP, Reinforcement Learning, Data Mining and more; other areas include databases, distributed systems, parallel computing, security, graphics, numerical computing and software engineering.

The rest: non-thesis students take 3 more courses at the 50000 or 60000 level, which can be outside CS, with at most 2 individual study courses; thesis students take 1 more course, which cannot be individual study. Courses on the plan of study must average at least 3.0, and one or two C-range grades are accepted only if offset by A-range grades. In the first year you also complete an ethics lecture and quiz, plus RCR training.

If you want to move into the PhD, you can petition. The department looks at whether you have completed the doctoral core requirements, whether you show research promise (for example, two semesters of CS 69800 or CS 59000), and whether a faculty member supports you.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

The CS department says CPT is not required for the degree, and notes that the guidance has changed, so contact ISS before applying. ISS rules:

- You generally need one full academic year of full-time enrollment first
- CPT of 20 hours a week or less is part-time, and more than 20 hours is full-time; 12 or more months of full-time CPT makes you ineligible for OPT
- Graduate students on CPT in Fall or Spring still need a normal registration, usually 8 credit hours
- Non-thesis master's students generally cannot do CPT in their final semester
- CPT is authorized semester by semester, and you must enroll in and complete the associated course or research credit

Under these rules, if you start in Fall, the earliest window is usually the summer after your first year.

## Application Requirements

- Deadlines: December 1, 2026 for Fall 2027 entry; October 1, 2026 for Spring 2027 entry, which has already passed. Fall offers go out before April 15, and Spring offers before December 1
- GRE: not required
- GPA: the Graduate School's program page lists a cumulative GPA of 3.5 (A=4.0) in the most recently conferred degree; the department says most successful applicants have major and cumulative GPAs above 3.5/4.0, 8.5/10 or 85/100
- English: the Graduate School's program page lists TOEFL 100 on the old format (22 in each section), 4.5 overall on the new TOEFL format from January 21, 2026 (Listening 5, other sections 4.5), IELTS 7 (7 in each band) and Duolingo 135 (135 in each subscore). The department's English page gives a higher recommendation based on past competitive applicants: IELTS 7.5 with no band below 7.0. The two sources do not fully match, so if in doubt, email csgradinfo@purdue.edu
- Recommendation letters: at least 3, from academic or professional references as recent as possible; if you have done research, the department expects a letter from your research supervisor
- Materials: transcripts, CS Supplementary Form, Academic Statement of Purpose, Personal History Statement and a resume
- Background: most applicants have a CS degree, though applicants from other fields with a suitable background are considered. The department expects knowledge of data structures, computer architecture, compilers, algorithm analysis, operating systems, distributed systems, networks and programming languages, math through differential equations, linear algebra and numerical methods, and proficiency in a language such as Java, C or C++

## Admissions Data (csgrad DP)

csgrad has 4 data points for Purdue MSCS, all for Fall entry, and all of them are rejections:

- 2023 Fall: 2 rejections
- 2024 Fall: 1 rejection
- 2025 Fall: 1 rejection

The 4 applicants came from four undergraduate categories, one each: a US school, a Canadian school, a 985 university, and UCAS/ShanghaiTech/SUSTech. Three majored in CS and one in software engineering. GPAs were 3.48–3.85 on a 4.0 scale, plus one 3.7 on a 4.3 scale. The two who reported English scores had TOEFL 100 and IELTS 7, and only one reported a GRE (318). Two had 1–2 domestic research experiences, and two had one internship each. Most recommendation letters were from course instructors; two applicants also had research letters.

None of these entries has notes, and with no admits in the sample, they say nothing about the admission bar.
