# Duke AIPI

## Program Overview

The degree is a Master of Engineering (MEng) from Duke's Pratt School of Engineering. The program is called AI for Product Innovation (AIPI) and sits under Pratt's Institute for Enterprise Engineering.

- Credits: 30 credits, made up of 10 courses plus 2 industry experiences (a company-sponsored industry project, and an internship or project)
- Length: the campus program normally takes 12 months (two semesters plus a summer session), or you can add one more fall semester and finish in 16 months. Classes are in Durham, North Carolina. There is also a 24-month part-time online version with the same degree, which includes 2 required residencies on campus
- Start: campus MEng programs in this cycle admit for Fall 2027. The summer before classes start, you first take a 4-week online bootcamp (AIPI 503, Python and data science math), which counts as a prerequisite. Students already proficient in Python and its data science libraries can ask to test out
- Tuition (2026-27 academic year): the 12-month track is $35,680 for each of two semesters plus $10,704 for the summer, $82,064 in tuition overall, and $123,132 for the full budget including housing, food and fees. The 16-month track is $90,984 to $99,904 in tuition, depending on how many credits you take in the final semester. The online version is $2,555 per credit, $76,650 in tuition overall
- STEM: neither the AIPI program page nor the Pratt admissions pages mention a STEM designation. If the OPT extension matters to you, email pratt_masters@duke.edu

AIPI combines AI with product and business coursework. Review the balance before applying: management, business and AI ethics are required alongside technical courses, so it may not fit someone focused exclusively on algorithm research.

## Courses and Course Selection

The 10 courses fall into three groups:

- Technical core: AIPI 510 Sourcing Data for Analytics, 520 Modeling Process & Algorithms, 540 Deep Learning Applications, 561 Operationalizing AI (MLOps), plus AIPI 501 Industry Seminar Series
- Product and business courses: AIPI 560 Legal, Societal & Ethical Implications of AI, MENG 540 Management of High-Tech Industries, and MENG 570 Business Fundamentals for Engineers. The program page says these were developed with Duke's law school and the Fuqua School of Business
- 3 electives: at least 1 must be an AIPI course, such as Large Language Models, Applied Computer Vision, Deep Reinforcement Learning Applications, Optimization in Practice, or Data Analysis at Scale in the Cloud. The rest can come from departments like ECE, STA, BME and CYBERSEC, for example ECE 682D Probabilistic Machine Learning or STA 663 Statistical Computation. Other Pratt graduate courses are open on a space-available basis with instructor permission

The capstone is AIPI 549: teams of 3–5 work on a company-sponsored project for one semester.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

- Every Pratt MEng requires an internship or project (MENG 550/551). The internship must be at least 320 hours, equal to 8 weeks full-time, and can be paid or unpaid. Instead of an internship, you can do a project approved by the AIPI program director
- Pratt runs a co-op: MEng and MEM students can work full-time at a company while staying enrolled, during the summer or the academic year. All co-op positions are paid, and you find the position yourself. The page does not say how this fits the AIPI schedule, so ask pratt_coop@duke.edu
- CPT: Duke Visa Services says that new CPT guidance from SEVP in August 2026 is still being evaluated and that Duke has temporarily paused CPT for most students. If you are on an F-1, ask Duke Visa Services whether your internship or co-op can use CPT

## Jobs and Location

The school is in Durham, North Carolina. The program's outcomes page reports that 96% of the Class of 2025 were employed or pursuing further education full-time, and that the average US base salary for the Classes of 2023–2025 was $124,562. The page does not give the number of respondents or a median. Common job titles are ML Engineer, AI Engineer and Data Scientist, and the employers listed include Google, Amazon, Microsoft and Nomura.

The outcomes page does not provide a survey sample size or median salary, so its figures should not be treated as outcomes for every graduate.

## Application Requirements

These are the requirements for the 2027 cycle (Fall 2027 entry):

- Deadlines: campus MEng Round 1 is November 3, 2026, Round 2 is January 19, 2027, and Round 3 is March 17, 2027. Online deadlines depend on status: March 17, 2027 for international applicants outside the US who need an F-1, and May 3, 2027 for US citizens, permanent residents, and people living in the US on an H-1B, L-1 or TN
- Work experience: the requirements page says it is not required for the AI program. The same page also says 12-month accelerated MEng programs are an exception, but the only program the deadlines page lists as needing 2 or more years of experience is the summer-start accelerated FinTech program
- GRE: optional for all 2027 applicants
- English: Duolingo is preferred, and TOEFL and IELTS are also accepted. There are no minimum scores. Admitted international students without a waiver must submit Duolingo scores by June 15 for language assessment and may be placed into free English courses
- Application fee: $75. No minimum GPA
- Prerequisites: an undergraduate degree in a STEM or STEM-adjacent field, or one with substantial STEM coursework; with a degree in another field, relevant technical work experience can count instead. You also need two semesters of calculus and at least one programming language, preferably Python. Probability/statistics and linear algebra are preferred but not required
- Materials: 3 recommendation letters (for the campus program, at least 1 academic letter for recent graduates; for the online program, at least 1 from work in industry); 3 short-answer questions of up to 1,500 characters each, covering why you are applying, a problem you want to solve with AI, and what sets you apart from other applicants; a 1–2 page resume; and a recorded video introduction with 30 seconds to prepare and up to 3 minutes to answer. Interviews are by invitation only and cannot be requested
