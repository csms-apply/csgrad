# UVA MCS

## Program Overview

The CS department in the University of Virginia (UVA) School of Engineering offers two master's degrees, and this page covers both:

- Master of Computer Science (M.C.S.): course-based, with a coursework-only option and an option that adds one small project
- Computer Science, M.S. (Master of Science): thesis-based; you write a master's thesis and defend it

Both degrees are 31 credits. In the degree inventory of the State Council of Higher Education for Virginia (SCHEV), both use CIP code 11.0101, which is on the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List (July 2024 version). The university is in Charlottesville, Virginia. For Engineering graduate students, full-time means at least 12 credits per fall or spring semester, with a maximum of 15. The time limit to finish is 7 years for the MCS and 5 years for the MS.

The 2026-27 tuition table in the UVA Graduate Record lists a separate row for the Computer Science Master's Program: $41,124 a year for non-Virginians and $23,652 for Virginians; when charged per credit, the non-Virginian rate is $2,285 per credit. The same table lists the comprehensive fee ($4,552 for non-Virginians in the regular session) and activity fees separately. Master's students can work as hourly-wage MTAs (teaching assistants), but the job comes with no tuition remission and no health insurance subsidy.

## MCS vs. MS

| | MCS (coursework) | MCS (project) | MS |
|---|---|---|---|
| CS 6190 (required) | 1 credit | 1 credit | 1 credit |
| Breadth electives | 12 credits | 12 credits | 12 credits |
| Other electives | 18 credits | 15 credits | 12 credits |
| Research | none | 3 credits of CS 7995 | 6 credits of CS 8999 thesis, over two semesters |
| Completion | submit three program assessment forms | written project description and a presentation to at least two CS faculty | written thesis and oral defense |

Officially, the research effort for the MCS project is equivalent to one course, and the MS thesis to two.

Advising also works differently. MCS students are assigned an academic advisor when they enter the program; MS students must first find a faculty member who agrees to advise them, and cannot enroll without an advisor. The School of Engineering requires you to spend at least one semester in the program you entered before you can apply to transfer to another program within Engineering.

## Courses and Course Selection

The course rules are the same for both degrees: generally only courses at the 6000 level and above count; with approval from your advisor and the Master's Graduate Program Director, up to 6 credits of non-CS 5000-level courses can count; courses graded below B don't count toward degree requirements.

The breadth electives must cover at least four of six areas, with at least 3 credits in each: cyber-physical systems/IoT/embedded systems; machine learning/NLP/data mining; security/privacy/cryptography; theory and algorithms; computer systems; and software engineering.

Among the courses listed in the 2026-27 Record, the ML area includes Machine Learning, Natural Language Processing and Reinforcement Learning; the systems area includes Cloud Computing, Computer Architecture, Operating Systems and Computer Networks; software engineering includes Compilers and Software Analysis and Applications. Many CS 6501 special topics courses also count toward different areas depending on the topic, for example GPU Architectures and Risks and Benefits of Generative AI and LLMs.

## Internships / CPT

The School of Engineering's graduate internship course, ENGR 8000, took effect on May 1, 2026. It is 1–12 credits, graded satisfactory/unsatisfactory. The internship description has to be approved by the SEAS Graduate Office first, and the instructor is usually your advisor. In the 2026-27 tuition table, Engineering MS and PhD internships are charged $1,452 per semester or $121 per credit.

The course description says it is for MS and PhD students only and does not mention the MCS. If you plan to do the MCS and intern during the term, check with the department and the international student office about CPT once you are admitted.

## Admission Requirements

The following are School of Engineering-wide rules from the 2026-27 Graduate Record:

- A bachelor's degree from a recognized institution is required
- GRE: optional
- Official transcripts aren't needed for review; you submit them when you matriculate
- English: TOEFL iBT 90 overall with 20 in each section (on the new scoring model, 4.5 overall, 4.5 in listening and 4.0 in the other sections); IELTS 6.5 in each section; Duolingo 120; scores must be no more than two years old from the test date to the date you submit your application. You are exempt if you earned your bachelor's degree entirely at an institution where English is the primary language of instruction
- Application fee: $0 for Engineering graduate programs in the 2026-27 fee table

For Fall 2027 deadlines, start terms and recommendation letter requirements, see the Engineering graduate admissions page (link at the end).

All 6 MCS applicants came from 985 universities in mainland China, majoring in CS, software engineering, or a CS-related interdisciplinary program. GPAs on a 4.0 scale ranged from 3.21 to 3.85, on a percentage scale from 86.99 to 90.6, and one was 4.33 on a 5.0 scale. TOEFL 96–105, IELTS 7. 4 reported GRE scores; the 3 who gave a total scored 324–325. In both 2023 and 2024, MCS decisions came out between March 12 and 14. Also, one MCS admit has a note saying MSCS, so it may be filed under the wrong degree.

The 5 MS applicants had more varied backgrounds: 985, 211, Sino-foreign joint programs and a Canadian undergrad, majoring in software engineering, AI and CS, plus one whose undergraduate major was not CS. Both 4.0-scale GPAs were 3.71; percentage-scale GPAs were 85.97–88; TOEFL 103–105, IELTS 7–8. 3 submitted GRE scores, 322–327; the two admits in 2025 and 2026 did not report one. All 4 admits listed research experience, while the one rejected applicant had no research and mainly internships. MS decisions came out between late February and early April. The sample is very small, so treat it as a rough reference.
