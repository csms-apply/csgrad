# USC CS28

## Program Overview
A two-year program, famously known for only looking at GPA. The class size is enormous (between CS28 and CS37), approaching nearly a thousand students.

## Admission
Only looks at background and GPA. Recommendation letters are optional. Rolling admissions. All applicants from all schools are pooled together and ranked by GPA, with offers sent from top to bottom. 985/211 university backgrounds are valued.

Internships and research are not considered. Recommended as a safety school for high-GPA candidates.

## RA / PhD Transfer Opportunities

Very difficult. USC CS-related programs have enormous class sizes, making it very hard to find RA opportunities, and transferring to a PhD is also very difficult.

## Courses and workload

CS28 is the old name. Since Fall 2024, the general non-thesis MSCS has been 32 units, one more course than before.

570 algorithms is worth taking: it's theory and proofs, not an interview-prep class. 571 Web is good for building development projects; the 2020–2022 version had web and mobile assignments, and the workload got fairly heavy in the later part. 402 OS is the hardcore option if you want to learn systems. 585 DB and 572 search are easier to pair with recruiting, although database material comes in handy on the job, so don't write it off as a pure easy course.

The same course number can feel very different with a different instructor; even 570 has had versions with different teaching styles and different homework-grading setups. A systems-heavy schedule and a light, recruiting-first schedule make for two very different ways to do this degree.

## Recruiting

The recruiting atmosphere is strong, but applications, referrals and LeetCode are mostly on you. There are career fairs and Handshake, and some people do get interviews from the fairs; being in LA alone won't get you extra ones, though. The 2022 new-grad market and 2025 were very different, and old stories about picking up big-tech offers easily aren't a realistic expectation now.

## Life

LA has tons of food and things to do, but having a car or not makes a big difference. Living near campus and cooking for yourself works fine without one; if you want to hit the Chinese neighborhoods and explore around regularly, a car makes life much easier. The lifestyle ceiling is high, and spending can rise right along with it.

## CPT — October 11, 2026 update

USC now requires practical training to be mandatory for every student in the program; an elective internship course isn't enough. The general MSCS has no published internship requirement for all students, and 597/598 don't count toward the degree, so the older CPT internship arrangements can't simply be reused.
