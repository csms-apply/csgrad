# UC Davis MSCS

## Program Overview

The degree is the Master of Science in Computer Science, offered by UC Davis's Graduate Group in Computer Science (GGCS), with the College of Engineering as lead college. The CS department describes the degree as preparing students to do research and to solve complex technological problems.

- Entry: Fall only; there is no spring admission
- Campus: the main Davis campus, on the quarter system
- Units and length: Plan I (thesis) is 30 units, and the FAQ on the MS page gives a typical 2–3 years; Plan II project is 36 units, about 2 years; Plan II exam is 36 units, about 1.5 years
- Cost: under the university's 2026-27 graduate fee schedule, full-time nonresidents pay $31,359.90 for three quarters, including $13,722 tuition and $15,102 nonresident supplemental tuition; student health insurance is $8,820 (you can apply for a waiver with comparable coverage); the international student administrative fee is $429 a year; new students also pay a one-time $150 document fee. That is about $40,609 before living costs, and the fee page notes the amounts may still change
- Funding: the CS FAQ says master's students generally do not receive an offer of support, and that the growth in PhD enrollment over the past two years has made TA and GSR positions much less likely for MS students. To TA for a CS course you must first complete ECS 390

## Courses

Admission expects undergraduate proficiency in three areas, each with a B or better: theory (ECS 120 or 122A), software (ECS 140A or 160) and operating systems (ECS 150 or 154B). You can be admitted with some of these missing and finish them before advancing to candidacy.

Graduate courses are grouped into three core areas, and you take at least one course in each, 12 units total:

- Theory: 220 Theory of Computation, 222A Algorithms, 240 Programming Languages, 256 Performance Evaluation
- Software: 231 Large-scale Scientific Computation, 235A Security, 245 Analysis of Software Artifacts, 260 Software Engineering, 261 Program Verification
- Systems: 201A Computer Architecture, 251 Operating Systems, 252 Computer Networks, 265 Distributed Database Systems

Electives are 12–24 units, including at least one non-core ECS 200-level course (not 289 or 299); at most 3 graduate courses outside ECS, at most 3 ECS 289 special-topics courses and at most 1 upper-division undergraduate course can count. Every course counted toward the degree needs a B or better. Full-time students take at least 12 units each quarter and at most 12 units of graduate courses, with ECS 299 research units filling the rest.

There are three ways to finish:

- Plan I thesis: 6 graduate courses, up to 6 units of ECS 299 count, and the advisor plus two other faculty form the committee that reviews the thesis
- Plan II project: 7 graduate courses, up to 8 units of ECS 299 count, and the advisor plus two other faculty form the committee; the advisor sets the deliverable, which can be a written report, an oral presentation or both
- Plan II exam: 9 graduate courses, no ECS 299 units count; the exam covers core-area material in a format set by the exam committee. You take it only in the quarter you graduate; it is offered in fall, winter and spring but not summer, and you can retake it once

Students usually pick a plan at the end of their third quarter and can switch before advancing to candidacy.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

The degree has no internship requirement. As of October 2026, the UC Davis international student office (SISS) says that, following new DHS guidance on CPT, the university has paused Course Credit CPT (the kind that is integral to study but not a degree requirement) and only continues to process degree-requirement CPT. The CS MS is not on its list of graduate programs that require an internship, so students in this program cannot get new CPT while the pause lasts. When it ends depends on future SISS updates.

The same page covers pre-completion OPT: you can apply after one full-time academic year, work up to 20 hours a week during the term and full time during breaks. Whatever period is authorized is deducted from post-completion OPT whether or not you actually work, with part-time counted at half.

## Jobs and Location

The campus is in Davis, Northern California. Graduate Studies says Sacramento is about 20 minutes away and the Bay Area is within a two-hour drive. The CS department's MS page has no employment data. Summer internships are affected by the CPT pause above; until it ends, pre-completion OPT is the option to look at.

## Application Requirements

- Deadline (Fall 2027): December 15, 2026. The application and transcripts must all arrive by then, and there is no priority deadline. The application opens each September; review starts in January, and most decisions go out by early spring
- GPA: the Graduate Studies minimum is 3.0/4.0, and applications below it are not considered
- GRE: the CS FAQ says it is neither required nor accepted, and scores sent anyway are not reviewed
- English: TOEFL iBT 80 (4.5/6 on the new scale for tests taken on or after January 21, 2026) or IELTS 7.0, valid for two years; you are exempt with a degree from a university whose sole language of instruction is English. Graduate Studies also accepts Duolingo 115, but the CS pages list only TOEFL and IELTS
- Materials: 3 letters of recommendation (submitted online), a Statement of Purpose, a Personal History Statement, a CV and transcripts. The application asks you to pick 1–4 research areas, which are used to route your file to faculty reviewers. The application fee is $155 for international applicants
- Competition: the CS FAQ says it reviews more than 2,000 applications a year and admits only about the top 10%; that figure covers the whole CS graduate group, with no separate number for the MS

## Admissions Results (csgrad DPs)

csgrad has 4 self-reported UC Davis MSCS records, all for Fall entry:

- 2022 Fall: 1 reject
- 2023 Fall: 1 admit, 1 reject
- 2025 Fall: 1 reject
- No 2024 or 2026 Fall records yet

That is 1 admit and 3 rejects in total. All four applicants came from mainland Chinese universities (one 985, one from the top-five "Huawu" group, two non-985/211), with two in CS and two in software engineering. GPAs on a 100-point scale ranged 88.8–92 (3 records), and one was 4.41 on a 5-point scale; TOEFL was 100–106 (3 records), and the 2 who reported GRE scores had 320–326 (the GRE is no longer accepted). All 3 who filled in their research background had research experience.

The only admit was a 2023 software engineering applicant from a non-985/211 school, 88.8 on a 100-point scale and ranked in the top 10%, TOEFL 101, with three domestic research projects and one domestic internship, two research letters and one course letter; the decision came in mid-April. All 3 rejections arrived between May and early June. The 2025 reject was also a non-985/211 CS applicant, with a 92 GPA and TOEFL 106, research assistant experience, non-first-author papers and competition awards, and all three letters marked strong. The sample is very small, so treat it as a rough picture only.
