# Waterloo ECE MEng

## Program Overview

Courses can help you explore different areas; if your goal is industry, also make time for relevant projects and work experience.

The full degree name is Master of Engineering (MEng) in Electrical and Computer Engineering, offered by the ECE department in Waterloo's Faculty of Engineering, with classes on campus. It is course-based and self-funded, with no supervisor and no department funding.

The same degree comes as two programs that you apply to separately:

- Regular MEng: 4 terms (16 months) full-time, or part-time over 8 terms. The department FAQ says it usually takes 4 terms and can be done in 3
- MEng Co-op: full-time only, with 1–2 paid work terms. The Fall 2026 graduate calendar and the graduate studies program page list 5–6 terms (20–24 months), while the department's co-op page says 16–20 months; go by the calendar

Both programs admit in September, January and May.

This is a Canadian program, so US STEM designation does not apply. IRCC states that since February 15, 2024, a master's program of at least 8 months qualifies for a 3-year PGWP even if it is shorter than two years; since November 1, 2024, most applicants also need to submit language test results.

Cost: on the Finance office's Fall 2026 and Winter 2027 international fee schedules, Engineering MEng full-time tuition is CAD 17,400 per term plus CAD 1,014.11 in incidental fees, about CAD 74,000 for 4 terms at that rate; part-time is CAD 8,700 per term. The co-op version adds a CAD 836 co-op fee, charged once in the first study term and again in each later work term.

## Courses and Course Selection

Under the Fall 2026 calendar, you need at least 8 graduate courses: at least 5 from ECE, and at most 3 from outside the department, which must come from Engineering, Mathematics or Science. Each course needs at least 65% and the cumulative average at least 70% to stay in the program. The co-op version has the same course requirements, plus WIL 601 Career Foundations for Work-Integrated Learning in the first study term.

You can add a Graduate Specialization, which appears on the transcript but not on the diploma. There are 10, each with at least 4 courses. The more CS-leaning ones:

- Software: required ECE 650 Methods and Tools for Software Engineering, ECE 651 Foundations of Software Engineering and ECE 653 Software Testing, Quality Assurance and Maintenance, plus 2 electives from a list including ECE 606 Algorithm Design and Analysis, ECE 656 Database Systems and ECE 751 Distributed and Network-Centric Computing
- Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning: required ECE 657 Introduction to Machine Learning and ECE 657A Introduction to Artificial Intelligence, plus 3 electives such as ECE 657D Neural Networks and Deep Learning or the ECE 750 Reinforcement Learning topic
- Computer Networking and Security: required ECE 610, ECE 628 Computer Network Security and ECE 655

On the hardware side there is Integrated Devices, Circuits, and Systems, with courses such as ECE 621 Computer Organization, ECE 627 Register-transfer-level Digital Systems and ECE 637 Digital Integrated Circuits, and its electives include the ECE 699A/699B MEng project courses. There is also Business Leadership (BE courses), which can be combined with one ECE specialization, raising the total to 9 or 10 courses. The calendar notes that electives are not guaranteed to run every year.

## Internship / CPT / Co-op

CPT is a US concept and does not apply here. The regular MEng has no work terms; if you want paid work built into the degree, you need to apply to the MEng Co-op:

- 1 or 2 work terms; 2 terms must be consecutive and with the same employer, and the program must start and end on a study term
- One Graduate WIL Reflective Report per work term, graded by the university's Centre for Work-Integrated Learning
- Students who do not find a co-op placement are moved back to the regular MEng
- The department says co-op is highly competitive with limited spots each term and recommends applying to both programs; once admitted to the regular MEng, neither new nor current students can transfer into co-op

On immigration, the university's international office posted in April 2026 that from April 1, 2026, a separate co-op work permit is no longer needed: a study permit with a condition allowing on-campus work is enough, since IRCC treats co-op placements as on-campus work. If your study permit lacks that condition, you need to request an amendment.

## Jobs and Location

The university is in Waterloo, Ontario. The department and graduate studies MEng pages do not publish employment data. The regular program has no work term in its structure; to stay in Canada after graduating, the route is the PGWP.

## Application Requirements

Both programs have the same requirements. The application fee is CAD 125 per program and cannot be waived.

- Deadlines: Fall 2027 is February 1, 2027; January entry closes June 1 of the previous year and May entry closes October 1 of the previous year. The department suggests starting your application at least two weeks before the deadline to give referees time; review is not first-come, first-served, and the FAQ estimates April decisions for fall entry
- Background: a relevant four-year honours bachelor's; applicants educated outside Canada need at least 75% overall standing or equivalent; for non-engineering degrees, relevance is assessed case by case, taking the statement and industry experience into account
- Materials: resume, statement of interest (describing the coursework you plan to take), program-specific questions, transcripts
- References: 2, at least 1 academic; academic referees must have directly taught you or supervised your research
- GRE: not required, but can be submitted
- English: TOEFL 4.5 (1–6 scale; writing 4.5, speaking, reading and listening 4 each), IELTS 6.5 (writing and speaking 6.0 each). Graduate studies rules say scores are valid for two years and must be met in a single sitting; TOEFL MyBest and IELTS One Skill Retake are not accepted. Three or more years of university study in Canada or an exempt country, among other cases, can exempt you

## Admissions Data (csgrad DP)

csgrad has 15 entries for the regular program and 17 for co-op, all from Fall 2023 and Fall 2024. One regular entry was recorded twice, leaving 14 after removing the duplicate.

| Program | Intake | Admit | Reject |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Regular MEng | Fall 2023 | 9 | 2 |
| Regular MEng | Fall 2024 | 3 | 0 |
| MEng Co-op | Fall 2023 | 2 | 12 (incl. 1 silent rejection) |
| MEng Co-op | Fall 2024 | 0 | 3 |

That is 22 applicants in total. 9 applied to both programs: 8 were rejected from co-op and admitted to the regular MEng, and 1 was rejected from both.

- 12 regular admits: 9 CS undergrads, and 1 each in software engineering, AI and data science; 6 from mainland China 985/C5 schools, others from Sino-foreign joint programs, Hong Kong and overseas universities, and 2 without a listed category. GPA 3.21–3.94 on a 4-point scale or 86.5–90.5 on a 100-point scale, ranks from top 1% to top 40%. TOEFL 96–108 (7 people), IELTS 6.5–7 (4 people), and the 3 who submitted GRE scored 321–328
- 2 regular rejections, with 4-point GPAs of 3.3 and 3.7
- 2 co-op admits: one Tsinghua/Peking EE, one 985 software engineering, with GPAs of 3.7/4 and 91.2/100; neither submitted GRE
- 15 co-op rejections: 8 from 985/C5 schools, 2 from 211 schools, the rest from non-985/211 mainland schools, Hong Kong, overseas and unlisted categories. GPA 3.3–3.95 on a 4-point scale or 86.5–93.05 on a 100-point scale, with 6 ranked in the top 3%; TOEFL 101–109, IELTS 6.5–7.5, GRE 320–334

Notes mention that a co-op rejection letter asked whether the applicant wanted to apply to the regular program instead, and that someone was advised to switch to the regular program and then admitted; another applicant felt they had submitted too late and recommended applying early.

The sample covers only two cycles, so treat it as a rough reference.
