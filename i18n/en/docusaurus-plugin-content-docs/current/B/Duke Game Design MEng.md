# Duke Game Design MEng

## Program Overview

The studio-style project is a central part of the degree: students work with a team to build a game over time. The first class graduated in 2026, so long-term outcomes remain limited. Look at the Demo Day projects and ask recent students how teams divide the work.

The degree is the Master of Engineering in Game Design, Development & Innovation; the program page also calls it the Masters in Game Development, Design & Innovation, or GDDI. It is part of the Pratt School of Engineering and uses the same degree framework as Duke's other MEng programs.

- Length: 30 credits over two years (four semesters); a typical Duke MEng takes three semesters
- Start term: the deadlines page lists only fall entry (Fall 2027); an optional bootcamp (GAMEDSGN 503, 0 credits) runs the summer before the program starts
- Campus: Duke's main campus in Durham, North Carolina
- The first class graduated in 2026; its Demo Day on May 4, 2026 showed capstone games from 4 student studios
- STEM: neither the program page nor the cost page lists a CIP code or a STEM designation, so ask the program directly if STEM OPT matters to you
- Cost (2026-27 cost page; the GDDI section notes its figures are estimates based on the 2027-28 academic year): the per-semester table lists tuition of $35,680 for each first-year semester, then $35,860 for the second-year fall and $10,704 for the second-year spring, which adds up to $117,924; the text on the same page estimates four-semester tuition at $115,960. The two figures do not match, so confirm with the school. Based on the per-semester table, the two-year budget including housing, insurance and living costs is $181,604. Internship courses carry no tuition

## Courses and Course Selection

The program is run like a game studio: students form teams and work on the same game for two years, taking it from an idea to a publishing-ready build.

The first year centers on C++ and Unreal:

1. Fall: GAMEDSGN 510 Programming for Game Development, GAMEDSGN 520 Fundamentals of Game Development and GAMEDSGN 530 Critical Analysis of Video Games, plus EGR 590 Career Strategy and Design (1 non-tuition credit)
2. Spring: GAMEDSGN 511 Software Engineering & Systems for Game Development, GAMEDSGN 521 Advanced Game Development and MENG 540 Management of High Tech Industries

In the fall of the second year you take two technical electives (plus 1–2 optional ones if you want) and the other required business course, MENG 570 Business Fundamentals for Engineers; the spring has only one course, GAMEDSGN 589 Game Development Capstone. The program encourages students to take as many GAMEDSGN electives as they can that fall. Game electives change every year. The program page lists game engine and tools development, AI in games, 3D animation and motion capture, networking and multiplayer, VR/AR, UX/UI, mobile game development and serious games. Qualified students can also take courses from other Pratt master's areas, such as AI, cybersecurity, ECE and FinTech.

The program page names C# and C++ as the languages, and Unreal and Unity as the engines.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

The internship is required: MENG 550 and MENG 551, 6 non-tuition credits in total, scheduled in the summer after the first year. Duke's general MEng rule is that internships usually last 8–12 weeks with at least 320 hours (equal to 8 weeks full-time), and can be paid or unpaid; with faculty adviser approval a project can replace the internship. The GDDI program page does not mention co-op.

International students should watch CPT closely. A Duke Visa Services notice dated September 3, 2026 says SEVP issued new guidance in August under which CPT must meet all three criteria: it is integral to the curriculum, it is required of all students in the program, and it is offered by an employer through a cooperative agreement. Visa Services' CPT page says Duke is evaluating the guidance and has temporarily paused CPT for most (though not all) students. The GDDI internship is required of every student, but whether a summer internship can still go through CPT as planned depends on Duke's decision, so ask the program directly when choosing schools.

## Jobs and Location

Duke is in North Carolina's Research Triangle. The program page lists nearby AAA and indie studios, including Epic Games, Insomniac Games and Red Storm Entertainment (Ubisoft); people from Epic Games and Scopely attended the first Demo Day.

The target roles named on the program page are gameplay engineer, tools/engine developer, technical designer, systems designer, producer and game UX. The employment figure on the site (nearly 9 in 10 graduates working or continuing their education within six months) covers all Duke MEng programs together. GDDI's first class only graduated in 2026, so there is no program-specific data yet.

## Application Requirements

- Deadlines (Fall 2027, shared by all Duke campus MEng programs): Round 1 November 3, 2026; Round 2 January 19, 2027; Round 3 March 17, 2027
- Background: any undergraduate major is accepted, but programming coursework is required; the site strongly prefers coursework in programming, object-oriented programming, data structures and algorithms, computer graphics, linear algebra and calculus. Work experience is not required
- GRE: optional for 2027 applicants
- English: there is no minimum score (and no minimum GPA or GRE either). Pratt prefers the Duolingo English Test but also accepts TOEFL and IELTS; admitted international students without a waiver must submit Duolingo scores by June 15 for English placement
- Recommendations: 3 letters, at least 1 of them academic
- Essays: two GDDI-specific short answers, each up to 1,500 characters including spaces. One asks what role you played when a creative or technical team you were on faced a major disagreement or breakdown; the other asks about a game that deeply affected you and why you chose this Duke program. The application also has a field for your portfolio URL
- Other: a 1–2 page resume; a timed video introduction with 30 seconds to prepare and up to 3 minutes to answer; a $75 application fee
