# OSU MSCSE

## Program Overview

If research or a PhD is your goal, compare the thesis track's advisor and research requirements with the coursework track's larger course load. Funding is focused on applicants interested in a PhD, so plan a master's budget on a self-funded basis.

This is the master's degree from the Department of Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) at The Ohio State University. The degree is the Master of Science in Computer Science and Engineering (M.S. CSE), with a thesis track and a coursework track.

- Campus: the main Columbus campus
- Credits: at least 30 for the thesis option (Research Track) and at least 33 for the coursework option. The department's sample plans spread the thesis option over 4 semesters and the coursework options over 3; the degree must be finished within 6 years
- Start term: international applicants are admitted for autumn only
- STEM: the university's graduate admissions page says international graduates of this major can apply for up to 3 years of work permission (OPT)
- Cost: under the registrar's 2026-27 tuition table, standard graduate programs on the Columbus campus charge per credit, capped at 8 credits, so full-time tuition and fees for nonresident students come to about $22,969 per semester. Engineering adds a $255 learning technology fee per semester, and new in-person MS/PhD students admitted in autumn 2026 or later pay a $500 program fee per semester, for about $23,724 per semester, or about $47,448 for two semesters (the table gives no total; these are calculated from the listed rates)
- Funding: the department considers applicants for associateships only if they plan to pursue a Ph.D., and nominates for fellowships only applicants who mark the "Ph.D. only" track, so plan to pay your own way as an MS student

## Courses and Course Selection

You don't pick a track when you enroll. You declare it when you apply to graduate, and you can switch along the way if you meet the new track's requirements. The coursework track has two options:

- Project: a project with a CSE faculty member, at least 6 credits over two consecutive semesters (CSE 6998, 6193 or 6999), a written report, and an oral exam of at least 60 minutes. This option requires a B average in the three Graduate Core courses
- Non-Project: no project, but a written exam at the end covering algorithms (required), either computability and complexity or programming languages, and either computer architecture or operating systems, 120 minutes each

Everyone takes:

- CSE 6891, the department research seminar, required in your first autumn (1 credit)
- Three Graduate Core courses: CSE 6331 Algorithms (required); one of 6321 Computability and Complexity or 6341 Programming Languages; one of 6421 Computer Architecture or 6431 Advanced Operating Systems
- Applied Core: at least 1 course for thesis students and at least 2 for non-thesis students, chosen from databases or data mining, compilers, parallel computing, network programming, machine learning or AI, and real-time rendering or data visualization
- Electives: any CSE course at the 5100 level or above. Graduate courses from other departments count for up to 6 credits, and courses from ECE, Math, Statistics, ACCAD and Linguistics need no extra approval

5000-level Pre-Core courses (such as software engineering, intro databases, intro operating systems, networking and survey of AI) count for at most 6 credits and do not count as electives.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

The CSE master's requirements include no internship or co-op course. For international students, CPT follows the rules of the university's Office of International Affairs (OIA):

- You must first complete one academic year of full-time study, and graduate students need a GPA of at least 3.0
- During the semester you can work at most 20 hours a week; during official breaks you can work full time
- CPT is approved one semester at a time, so a position that spans terms needs a new application
- The training has to be a required part of the curriculum; if it is only optional, OIA says to use OPT instead
- Graduate students holding a GA, TA or RA appointment are generally not eligible for CPT during the academic year

The OIA page also notes that a new federal immigration rule was paused by a preliminary court injunction on September 14, 2026, so this information may still change. Whether master's students can use CPT for a summer internship is a question for the department and OIA.

## Application Requirements

- Deadline: the Autumn 2027 application opens August 3 and closes December 15 (that is, December 15, 2026). The department says autumn reviews wrap up in March to April
- GPA: minimum 3.0/4.0; the department says most applicants have about 3.5 or higher
- GRE is not required
- English: CSE sets no scores of its own and uses the Graduate School minimums: TOEFL iBT 79 (4.0 on the new TOEFL introduced in January 2026), IELTS 6.5 or Duolingo 120. MyBest scores are not accepted
- Materials: statement of purpose, a CV of no more than two pages, 3 recommendation letters, official transcripts and proof of degree from every institution attended, and the application fee. The department currently offers no application fee waivers
- Background: applicants are expected to have a CS bachelor's degree, or to have taken calculus and discrete math, two intro programming courses, data structures and algorithms, systems programming, computer architecture, and formal languages or programming languages

## Admissions Results (csgrad DP)

csgrad has 5 data points for OSU MSCSE:

- 2023 Fall: 2 admits, 0 rejects
- 2024 Fall: 1 admit, 1 reject
- 2025 Fall: 1 admit, 0 rejects

The 4 admits came from four different undergraduate categories: a US undergraduate school, a 985 school, a non-985/211 school, and the UCAS/ShanghaiTech/SUSTech group. Their majors were CS, ECE and software engineering. GPAs were 3.50–3.68 on a 4.0 scale, or about 89 on a 100-point scale (top 10%). The two who reported test scores had TOEFL 101–103 and GRE 320–321 (Quant 166–169), with 2–3 domestic research projects and 1 domestic internship each. Another admit had a top-venue paper (not first author) and a first-author top-venue paper under submission. The ECE admit noted taking many CS courses to cover the prerequisites.

The 1 reject was a CS student from a "Top 5" Chinese university with a GPA of about 85/100 (below the top 50%), GRE 321 (Quant 167), TOEFL section scores adding up to 109, research with one advisor, and two papers under submission at application time (one as first author).

With only 5 data points, treat them as a rough reference.
