# NEU IS

## Program Overview
A career-change-to-CS program that offers co-op opportunities. It's similar to MSCS, and the I-20 lists SE (Software Engineering), which is a CS-related title.

There are multiple campuses.
If you want to experience a traditional American college campus life, are young and not in a rush to find a job, and have a strong overall profile, go to the Boston campus. If your goal is landing a job at a big tech company, or you can't stand the Boston winters, come to the Silicon Valley campus. The GRE is not required for 2025-2026 entry, and the College of Engineering has waived the MS application fee for Spring 2027 and Fall 2027 entry.

Normally it takes two years to graduate, but if you do an internship or co-op in between, it may take about two and a half years.

NEU IS is essentially a career-change-friendly program. Its curriculum is close to NEU MSCS, but the admission bar is lower. Its core selling points:

1. The I-20 lists SE (Software Engineering), a CS-related title, which is very friendly for SDE job hunting — HR won't screen you out for a mismatched title.
2. Co-op is supported, so you can do a full-time internship in the fall, which gives you several more chances to land an offer than schools that only allow summer internships.
3. It's a STEM program, so after graduation you get OPT plus the STEM OPT extension, about 3 years of work authorization in total.

## Admission Threshold
There are even many Ivy League students from other majors who come to NEU to transition into CS. When applying you can check MSCS / CS Align / IS / SES at the same time (the application fee is currently waived, and one application covers up to 3 programs).

That said, the admission threshold is not high and is quite friendly.

## Curriculum

- 32 credits in total; the only required core course is INFO 5100 (4 credits, with the 0-credit INFO 5101 Lab); the Coursework option adds 16 credits of INFO restricted electives
- The rest are electives; if you want to move into software, choose Software Development. Available tracks include:
  - Cyber and Information Security
  - Product Management & DevOps Leadership
  - Software Testing and Quality Assurance
  - UX Design / Software Development / Data Analytics, etc.
- Most NEU courses are graded fairly leniently, which makes it very suitable for doing LeetCode and job hunting while taking classes. For example, Mobile Application 5520 and Computer Networks 5700 are widely considered easy courses.

## IS Bridge (Non-STEM Backgrounds)

If you applied to regular IS but received a Bridge offer, check the offer, campus and curriculum before asking the program office about a change. Include the extra credits in your budget.

Bridge is a separate version of IS, officially the Master of Science in Information Systems—Bridge, for students whose bachelor's degree is in a non-STEM, nontechnical field. The 2026-2027 catalog lists a Bridge version at 7 campuses (Boston, Arlington, Portland, Seattle, Silicon Valley, Toronto and Vancouver) plus Online.

The 5 US campus versions (Boston, Arlington, Portland, Seattle and Silicon Valley) share the same requirements. Compared with regular IS, the differences are mainly in credits and required courses:

- 36 semester hours in total, or 37 with co-op (the extra hour is ENCP 6000); regular IS is 32 (33 with co-op)
- 12 hours of required core: INFO 5001 Application Modeling and Design, INFO 5100 (with the INFO 5101 Lab), and INFO 6215 Business Analysis and Information Engineering, each with a grade of C or higher
- 12 hours of restricted electives from courses such as DAMG 6210 Data Management and Database Design, INFO 6205 Program Structure and Algorithms, INFO 6150 Web Design and User Experience Engineering, and INFO 7245 Agile Software Development
- The remaining 12 hours are open electives, mainly CSYE (except CSYE 6220), DAMG, INFO, or TELE courses
- A minimum 3.0 GPA to graduate

Toronto and Vancouver have the same total hours and core, but you pick either a Project or a Thesis option, both of which include INFO 7945 Master's Project, and electives come from a fixed list. The Online version has a different structure: 40 semester hours, mostly prescribed courses, and INFO 5001 and INFO 5002 (Introduction to Python) must be passed with a B or higher.

csgrad doesn't have any DPs tagged as Bridge yet. The 6 existing IS DPs (5 for Fall 2023 and 1 for Fall 2025, all admits) all come from CS or software engineering undergrads, so they say little about Bridge applicants from non-STEM backgrounds.
