# UNC MSIS

## Program Overview

The full degree name is Master of Science in Information Science, offered by the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. The school was called the School of Information and Library Science (SILS); its website now uses the name School of Data and Information Sciences (SDIS), and sils.unc.edu redirects to sdis.unc.edu. The 2026-27 tuition table still uses the old name. The program page says it is accredited by the American Library Association (ALA). The school comes from library and information science, not from a CS department.

The program is taught on campus in Chapel Hill. You need 48 credits to graduate, and the school reports an average time to graduation of 2 years for MSIS (classes of 2019–2023). The admissions page lists a single deadline and says nothing about spring entry.

The website does not say whether the program is STEM-designated. UNC ISSS says the CIP code is printed on page one of the I-20, and it must be on the DHS STEM list for a STEM OPT extension. Check your I-20 after admission, or ask ISSS directly.

Tuition is charged per credit. For 2026-27, MSIS out-of-state tuition is $1,964.50 per credit, capped at $17,680.50 per semester at 9 or more credits. Full-time students also pay $1,063 in mandatory fees each semester, plus student health insurance (you can request a waiver if you have other coverage). Two semesters of tuition and mandatory fees come to about $37,487 per academic year, not counting insurance. The tuition table notes that rates are not final until approved by the NC System Board of Governors.

The program page mentions merit-based support for incoming students and several assistantships each year that include full tuition.

## Courses and Course Selection

The 48 credits break down like this:

- Two core courses: INLS 776 Ethics, Values, and Society (spring) and INLS 777 Perspectives on Information, Technology, and People (fall), 3 credits each
- Six bin courses, 18 credits in total: at least 2 in Information, at least 2 in Technology, at least 1 in Services and Organizations, and at least 1 in People and Communities
- Six electives, 18 credits: more bin courses, courses recommended for a specialization, or graduate courses in other departments. Courses outside the school cannot count toward the bins, only as electives; inter-institutional registration and WISE courses can also count
- A 6-credit capstone, one of two options: a research-based master's paper, or a practicum project where a team solves a real problem for a local organization. You also have to pass a comprehensive exam

On the technical side, the 2026-27 graduate catalog lists INLS 523 databases, INLS 541 information visualization, INLS 570 fundamentals of programming information applications, INLS 572 web development, INLS 613 Text Mining, INLS 642 Data Mining, INLS 718 User Interface Design and others.

Specializations are informal and do not appear on the transcript. The technical ones include Data Science/Data Analytics/Databases, Information Retrieval and Text Mining, User Experience and HCI, and Health Informatics. The only formal concentration is Archives and Records Management. Certificates that do appear on the transcript are Biomedical Imaging Science, Digital Humanities and Public Health Informatics.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

The program page lists an optional 135-hour professional field experience. It is not a graduation requirement. The program page does not mention co-op.

As of October 2026, the UNC ISSS CPT page says CPT processes are being revised and gives no specific rules; ISSS posted CPT-related notices in August and October. Whether an optional internship can be approved for CPT is something to confirm with the program and ISSS before you enroll.

## Jobs and Location

The school is in Chapel Hill, North Carolina. Past graduate positions listed on the program page include UX Designer at SAS, Machine Learning Lead at EBSCO, Data and Policy Analyst at Acumen and Analyst at Credit Suisse, as well as Metadata Librarian at a university library.

The school's published data: 84% of 2023 MSIS graduates held a position in the field within 12 months (82% response rate), and the figure ranged from 83% to 91% for 2019–2023. International students are not broken out.

csgrad does not have any job-search records for this program yet.

## Application Requirements

These are the requirements on the official website as of October 2026:

- Deadline: February 9, 2027. To be considered for Graduate School and school funding, apply by December 1, 2026
- GRE: not required or recommended; you may submit scores voluntarily if you think they help
- English: international applicants submit TOEFL, IELTS or Duolingo. The Graduate School's recommended minimums are TOEFL 90 for tests taken before January 26, 2026 and 5 on the new TOEFL scale for tests taken on or after that date, IELTS 7, and Duolingo 110; scores must be no more than two years old. The school's pages do not set higher minimums
- Materials: 3 letters of recommendation, a statement of interest, a resume and transcripts. The application fee is $95, with waivers for certain groups
- The school says recently admitted graduate students had an average undergraduate GPA of 3.5/4.0. That figure covers the whole school, not just MSIS
