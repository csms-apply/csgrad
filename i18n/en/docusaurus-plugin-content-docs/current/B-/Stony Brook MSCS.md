# Stony Brook MSCS

## Program Overview

The degree is the M.S. in Computer Science, offered by the Department of Computer Science at Stony Brook University, part of the College of Engineering and Applied Sciences (CEAS). The department describes it as an applied, industry-oriented program focused on programming, software development, computer systems and applications.

You need 31 graduate CSE credits with a cumulative GPA of at least 3.0, and every course you count must be a C or better. You can start in fall or spring. Full-time is 9 credits per fall or spring semester. The sample schedule in the department's graduate handbook takes two years; students with excellent preparation may finish in three semesters (not counting summer).

The same handbook also applies to the SUNY Korea campus in Incheon. This page covers only the main campus in New York.

The department states that MS students are not normally considered for funding or tuition waivers at admission. A small number get partial support from the department or the university.

Tuition is charged per credit. For Fall 2026, the graduate non-New York resident rate is $1,158 per credit. A 9-credit semester comes to $10,422 in tuition, with fees on top. At that rate, tuition alone for 31 credits comes to about $35,900. Students who are not U.S. citizens or permanent residents are charged a separate international student health insurance rate, and can apply for a waiver if they have comparable personal coverage.

## Courses and Course Selection

Breadth requirement: at least one course from each of three groups, Theory, Systems, and Information and Intelligent Systems. Theory includes 512 Machine Learning and 548 Analysis of Algorithms. Systems includes 502 Computer Architecture, 506 Operating Systems, 532 Database Systems and 535 Distributed Systems. The third group includes 519 Data Science Fundamentals, 527 Computer Vision, 537 Artificial Intelligence and 538 Natural Language Processing.

Pick one of three project options:

- Basic Project: 9 graduate lecture courses in total, one of them from the Basic Project list (project-heavy courses such as 506, 532, 535, 537, 538 and 545). There is nothing to declare and no faculty advisor. The handbook says this option was made simpler starting Fall 2025
- Advanced Project: 7 lecture courses plus CSE 523/524, a two-semester project with the same faculty member
- Thesis: 6 lecture courses plus 6–9 credits of CSE 599, with the thesis approved by a committee of at least three CS faculty

If you choose Advanced Project or Thesis, you need an advisor by the start of your second semester. You don't have to declare an option in advance and you can switch, but if you change advisors or move into or out of Basic Project, the project part starts over. Credits you already earned still count toward graduation.

To have the Data Science and Engineering concentration on your transcript, take 4 of 512, 519, 537, 544, 545, 548 and 564, and complete 523/524 or a thesis.

Admits without a CS background may have proficiency requirements listed in the admission letter (CSE 581–583 bridge courses or CSE 587). These don't count toward the lecture course requirement, and the department normally won't approve CPT until they are done. If you work as a TA, 3 credits of CSE 698 can replace one lecture course.

## Internships / CPT

The handbook (Fall 2025 edition, revised July 2026) says an internship is not required to graduate. For MS students, CPT has to go with CSE 596 (the internship course), CSE 523/524 or CSE 599. VIS requires two full fall/spring semesters, a GPA of 3.0 or higher and no incomplete grades. CPT during fall or spring is approved only in special circumstances. Once you accept an internship offer, you must report the employer to the department within 7 days. A substantiated case of reneging earns a U in the internship course and no further CPT.

The VIS CPT page says SEVP issued new guidance on August 12 and August 24, 2026: the practical training must be something the program requires of all students, and if you can graduate without it, it generally does not qualify for CPT. The MSCS internship is not required, so under this rule it is unclear whether an ordinary summer internship can still get CPT. This is the situation as of October 2026; ask the department and VIS before you enroll.

STEM OPT extension eligibility depends on the CIP code on your I-20. VIS asks students to check it against the DHS STEM list themselves.

## Jobs and Location

The campus is on Long Island's North Shore, about 60 miles from New York City according to the university. The LIRR Port Jefferson line runs from Penn Station in Manhattan to Stony Brook station at the north end of campus, usually with a transfer at Jamaica or Huntington.

## Application Requirements

These are the requirements listed on the official sites as of October 2026:

- Deadline: March 15, 2027 for Fall 2027, for both domestic and international applicants. The program also admits in spring; the Spring 2027 international deadline of October 1, 2026 has passed. Late applications are not accepted
- GRE: required for international MS applicants, unless you hold a degree from a U.S. or Canadian institution. The ETS code is 2548, and official scores must arrive by the deadline
- English: the Graduate School's minimum for master's applicants is TOEFL 80 (tests taken before January 21, 2026) or 4.5 on the new TOEFL (tests taken after that date), or IELTS 6.5 overall with no section recommended below 6. A degree from an English-medium institution exempts you
- Materials: 3 recommendation letters (academic or industry), a statement of purpose, a resume, all transcripts and scans of degree certificates; the application fee is $100
- Accepting the offer requires a $500 tuition deposit
- Applicants with non-standard credentials but clear potential are occasionally given provisional admission, which requires assigned remedial courses in the first semester with at least a B average

## Admissions Results (csgrad DPs)

csgrad has only 1 DP (recorded as SBU MSCSE in the database): a Fall 2023 reject.

The applicant came from a mainland China university in the "Huawu" top-five group, majored in software engineering, had a 3.41/4.3 GPA (ranked below the top 50%), TOEFL 98, no GRE on record, and two internships in China. In the notes, the applicant guessed the reason might be low grades in a few major courses entered on the application. With a single data point, this tells you little.
