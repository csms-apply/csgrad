# VT MEng CS

## Program Overview

This is the coursework master's from Virginia Tech's Department of Computer Science. The full degree name is Master of Engineering in Computer Science and Applications.

- Length: 30 credits, no thesis. Full-time students can finish in as little as 3 semesters; the department page says part-time study is also an option.
- Intake: fall and spring, but the two-semester project path is fall entry only.
- Campus: most students take classes at Academic Building One in Alexandria (the VT Institute for Advanced Computing), three blocks from the Potomac Yard-VT Metro station and just outside Washington, D.C. The MEng on the main Blacksburg campus is limited to current and former VT students, VT employees and students from partner institutions, so most applicants end up in Alexandria.
- Tuition (2026-27 academic year, full-time, per year): in Alexandria, non-resident tuition is $35,942 and fees are $3,276, $39,218 in total. The school's living-cost estimate for Alexandria (housing, food, course materials, personal expenses and transportation) adds up to about $33,000, including $20,700 for housing. The application fee is $75.
- Scholarships: the Regional scholarship listed on the website is only for D.C. and Maryland residents, and the Boeing scholarship is only for U.S. citizens and permanent residents.
- STEM: the program page doesn't say. VT's international student center notes that your major's CIP code is printed on page 1 of your I-20, and you can check it against the DHS list of STEM fields.

## Courses

Software engineering coursework can overlap with professional development experience and draw on mathematical foundations such as linear algebra. Choose courses around your background and areas you want to strengthen.

Under the current curriculum page, you pick one of two paths:

- Project Enhanced: required CS 5024 Ethics & Professionalism in CS and CS 5934 Capstone Design, 3 credits each. You need 18 credits completed before taking the capstone.
- Project Intensive: CS 5925/5926 Integrated Project Design over two semesters, 6 credits each.

Both paths also require CS 5040 Intermediate Data Structures and Algorithm Design (3 credits). Students with a CS undergraduate degree or minor can usually have it waived at admission and take another graduate elective instead. The rest is electives: 21–24 credits on Project Enhanced, 15–18 on Project Intensive. Projects can come from companies, government agencies or non-profits, or you can propose your own; past partner companies listed on the project page include Boeing, Northrop Grumman, CoStar and Bloomberg Government.

Electives are grouped into focus areas. The website says most students choose two, and each area has three core courses, for example:

- Applied AI and Machine Learning: CS 5740 AI Tools for Software Engineering, CS 5805 Machine Learning, CS 5834 Introduction to Urban Computing
- Software Systems: CS 5244 Web Application, CS 5274 Cloud Computing, CS 5704 Software Engineering
- Computer Systems and Architecture: CS 5204 Operating Systems, CS 5504 Computer Architecture, CS 5565 Network Architecture and Protocols

The other three areas are Cybersecurity, Human-Centered Computing and Emerging Technologies.

Limits: at most 6 credits of 4000-level undergraduate courses; everything must be CS except one approved course from another department; CS 5904 Project and Report, CS 5974 Independent Study, CS 5994 thesis research and similar courses don't count toward the MEng.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

The degree requirements don't include an internship.

VT's international student center (Cranwell) currently has a notice on its CPT page: after SEVP issued two Broadcast Messages (2608-01 and 2608-02) on August 12 and 24, 2026, Cranwell paused new CPT authorizations. It now only processes CPT for programs where practical training is required, appears on the major's official graduation checklist, and is required of every student. CPT already authorized for the current semester is not affected, and the list of qualifying programs is still being compiled. Whether a summer internship after you enroll can go through CPT will depend on the policy at that time.

The general requirements listed on the page are one full academic year (two semesters) of full-time enrollment, a GPA of at least 3.0 for graduate students, an offer letter, and enrollment in GRAD 5944 or a department internship course; CPT is approved semester by semester. On a 3-semester schedule, that usually means the summer after the first year.

## Jobs and Location

Alexandria is in the D.C. metro area. The program page says nearby employers include federal agencies, government contractors, technology companies, research organizations and startups, working in AI, cybersecurity, software, defense and infrastructure. The website gives no placement rate, salary data or employer list.

## Application Requirements

- Deadline (Fall 2027): March 1, 2027, with rolling decisions; domestic applicants may also apply on a rolling basis as space allows, and applying early is recommended for scholarship consideration. The Spring 2027 deadline for international applicants was September 1, 2026, and has passed.
- Background: GPA of at least 3.0 and prior programming experience; your transcript needs at least one course equivalent to VT's CS 2114 or CS 5020. CS 5040 relies heavily on Java, and the website strongly advises being proficient in Java before you start.
- GRE: not required.
- English: the CS department requires TOEFL 90 with 20 in each section, or IELTS 6.5. For the new TOEFL taken on or after January 21, 2026, the Graduate School requires 4.5 overall and 4.5 in each section. If you hold a bachelor's degree from an accredited university where English was the medium of instruction, the Graduate School waives the requirement after reviewing your transcript.
- Recommendation letters: 1.

## Admission Results (csgrad Data Points)

csgrad has 3 records, all Fall 2023 admits, from 3 applicants. They are self-reported and don't say which campus. There are no records for the 2024–2026 cycles.

Backgrounds: 2 from the "Huawu" top-five mainland Chinese universities and 1 from a non-985/211 school; 2 CS majors and 1 software engineering major. GPA was 3.1–3.3 on a 4-point scale, and one 3.41 on a 4.3 scale; one ranked around the top 30% and two were below the 50% mark. TOEFL 98–113. Two sent GRE scores (327 and 334); one did not. Two had internships in China, and the third had several years of full-time work experience plus research experience in China.

That's three people from a single cycle, so treat it as a rough reference only.

## Comparison with Similar Programs

- [UVA MCS](../B/UVA%20MCS.md): another public-university coursework CS master's in Virginia; see that guide for its credit structure and tuition. The VT MEng differs in that classes are mainly next to D.C., and both paths require a project.
- [UMD MSCS](../A/UMD%20MSCS.md): also in the D.C. area, but more research-oriented: even the non-thesis option requires a scholarly paper, which the department says should mirror the standards of a conference or journal paper. The VT MEng is coursework plus a project, and research courses don't count toward it.
