# UCI NetSys

## Program Overview

The degree is the M.S. in Networked Systems, run jointly by the CS department in UC Irvine's Bren School of Information and Computer Sciences and the EECS department in the Samueli School of Engineering. The program site says it is a degree program, not a separate department, and its focus is computer and telecommunication networks. The same program also admits PhD students; you choose M.S. or Doctoral in the online application.

- Campus and length: main Irvine campus, quarter system. The site does not give a standard length, but the core seminar NetSys 295 has to be taken in Fall, Winter and Spring of the same academic year, so you need at least one full academic year.
- Start term: the online application only takes Fall entry; Winter or Spring starts are accepted only in exceptional, pre-arranged cases.
- Size: the Students page currently lists 20 MS students; the MS graduate list shows 29 graduates in 2024, 15 in 2025 and 9 in 2026.
- Cost: NetSys is not on the Registrar's 2026-27 lists of programs with professional degree supplemental tuition or self-supporting fees, so it follows the standard fee schedule for state-supported graduate programs. For 2026-27, a nonresident pays $38,784.44 for the three quarters, including $13,722 tuition, $15,102 nonresident supplemental tuition and $7,771.86 student health insurance. Fees for 2027-28 have not been published yet.
- Funding: the site says a limited number of fellowships, RAships and TAships are available to new students; any offer is stated in the admission letter.
- STEM: neither the program site nor the 2026-27 UCI catalogue gives a CIP code, so STEM OPT eligibility depends on the CIP code on your I-20.

## Courses

The MS has two plans:

- Plan I (thesis): 10 courses plus a thesis. 3 core courses, 2 breadth courses (at most 1 from the management list), 3 concentration courses (covering at least 2 concentrations) and 2 courses approved by your advisor; you also take at least two quarters of CS 298 or EECS 296 and give an oral presentation of the thesis to a three-member committee.
- Plan II (comprehensive exam): 12 courses. 3 core courses, 3 breadth courses (at most 2 from the management list), 4 concentration courses (covering at least 3 concentrations) and 2 courses approved by your advisor. The comprehensive exam runs through NetSys 295 as a term paper on a topic related to your program and that term's speakers.

The core courses are NetSys 201 (Computer and Communication Networks), NetSys 202 (Networking Laboratory) and the NetSys 295 seminar. The site says NetSys 202 is not offered this year and a substitution policy will be set; for 2026-27, students are also expected to attend 10 talks from the CS or EECS seminar series.

Breadth courses come in two groups: CS and engineering courses such as cryptography, databases, architecture, algorithms, probability models and AI; and management and applications of technology, such as usable security and privacy, management of information technology and network theory. Concentration courses fall into four groups, Networks, Performance, Middleware and Communications, covering advanced networks, wireless networking, network security, distributed systems, optimization, machine learning, middleware, advanced operating systems, digital communications, information theory, signal processing and more.

Substituting another course for a breadth or concentration course needs approval from your advisor and the program director. You can petition to transfer at most 2 graduate courses taken at another university before UCI, if they did not count toward another degree and you got a B or better.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

The degree requirements include no internship or co-op. The UCI International Center's current CPT page says CPT is approved only for practical training required for the degree, not for optional internships; among the programs it lists as having an internship degree requirement, the only one in the ICS school is MSWE, and NetSys is not listed. The other category is thesis-track master's students who have advanced to candidacy, with a letter from the advisor confirming the internship is a required part of the thesis; NetSys MS students advance to candidacy one quarter before the degree is conferred.

CPT also requires one full academic year of enrollment first, and you can work only 20 hours a week or less during the quarter and full time during breaks. Plan II does not fit either category. If you want an off-campus internship while enrolled, ask your advisor and the International Center early after you arrive.

## Jobs and Location

The campus is in Irvine, in Orange County, Southern California. The program publishes no MS employment data; the Students page only lists where PhD graduates went, including Google, Meta, Microsoft, Apple and Juniper Networks.

## Application Requirements

- Deadline (Fall 2027): January 15, 2027. The body of the application page still carries older Fall 2026 wording (materials accepted until January 31 after the priority deadline), so go by the application system.
- Background: a bachelor's in CS, computer engineering or EE is expected; applicants from other fields may need supplementary coursework.
- GRE: not required.
- English: UCI Graduate Division minimums are TOEFL iBT 80 (4 on the new scale starting January 2026) or IELTS 7 overall with no module below 6; scores two years old or older are not accepted. The program page says applicants who completed a B.S. or M.S. in the United States are exempt.
- Materials: transcripts, Statement of Purpose, Personal History Statement and at least 3 letters of recommendation; the application fee for international applicants is $155.
- Decisions go out from February through May. Admitted MS students may be eligible for the on-campus graduate housing waitlist, with a May 1 deadline.

## Admissions Data (csgrad DPs)

csgrad has 6 self-reported UCI NetSys records, all for Fall entry:

- 2023 Fall: 4 records, 3 admits and 1 reject
- 2024 Fall: 1 record, admit
- 2025 Fall: 1 record, admit

Five applicants did their undergrad in mainland China (2 from the "Huawu" top-five group, 2 from 985 schools, 1 from a non-985/211 school) and one in continental Europe; majors include CS, AI, ECE and information security. GPAs: 3.37–3.92 on a 4.0 scale (3 applicants), 87–89.1 on a 100-point scale (2 applicants), and one on a 30-point scale, ranked in the top 10%. TOEFL 101–109; the 3 applicants who reported GRE all had 328. The only reject had the lowest 4.0-scale GPA, 3.37.

One admitted applicant noted no networking experience beyond coursework. Five of the six each applied to at least 13 programs, and the 2 who recorded a final destination both went to other programs. There are no Fall 2026 records yet; the sample is small, so treat it as a rough reference.
