# Rutgers MSCS

## Program Overview

The full degree name is Master of Science in Computer Science. It is offered by the Department of Computer Science at Rutgers–New Brunswick, with admissions and enrollment handled by the School of Graduate Studies–New Brunswick. The department is on Busch Campus, at 110 Frelinghuysen Road, Piscataway.

You need 36 credits to graduate, i.e. 12 three-credit courses. The official FAQ says the program takes two years, or four semesters, of full-time study. You can start in either fall or spring.

Full-time is 9 credits (3 courses) per semester. To take 4 courses in one semester you need a GPA of at least 3.5, no grade below B, and approval from the program office first; 4 courses is the cap.

The program pages do not state a STEM designation or CIP code; the Rutgers Global OPT page only says to check with its office about STEM OPT eligibility.

The university and the department do not fund MS students. Some research groups occasionally fund individual students from their research grants.

Tuition is charged per credit. For 2026-27, the out-of-state rate at the School of Graduate Studies–New Brunswick is $1,595 per credit, so 36 credits come to about $57,420. Full-time students also pay a $964 student fee, a $233.50 school fee and a $221 technology fee each semester. F-1 students pay a $500 International Student Fee each semester, and the student health insurance plan (SHIP) is $1,378 in fall and $1,908 in spring.

## Courses and Course Selection

The 12 courses break down like this:

- 2 from Category A: algorithms and theory, e.g. 512/513 Data Structures and Algorithms, 509 Foundations of Computer Science, 521 Linear Programming
- 2 from Category B: AI/ML, computer architecture, databases, programming languages, robotics, systems/networks and visualization, e.g. 518 Operating Systems Design, 545 Distributed Systems, 552 Computer Networks, 539 Database Management Systems, 536 Machine Learning
- 4 more from Category A or B: 2 of these can be replaced by the thesis, or be the project courses you use for the essay
- 4 from the approved list: approved courses from other departments, more Category A/B courses, CS seminars, some undergraduate courses, or independent study

At most 12 credits can come from outside the CS department. The official list includes graduate courses in ECE, statistics, mathematics, industrial engineering and other departments. For a course not on the list, you have to email the program for approval.

The six concentrations (General CS, ML/AI, Massive Data Analytics, Robotics, Systems/Security, Vision/Visualization/Graphics) are only course templates. They have no required courses and do not appear on the degree.

You must submit either an essay or a thesis to graduate; the Capstone is no longer offered. The essay can be a course project report, as long as you got a B or higher on that project, and it has to come with presentation slides. The thesis means registering for 6 credits of 198:704–706 and is reviewed by a committee of your supervisor plus two other faculty members.

You need a GPA of at least 3.0 and no more than 2 grades of C or lower.

## Internships / CPT

The department's FAQ says an internship is not required to graduate. The CPT rules in that FAQ are: you need 18 credits (two semesters) before you can apply; during fall and spring CPT can only be part-time, at most 20 hours a week, and requires a GPA of 3.0 or higher; full-time CPT is only allowed in the summer. The department's Practical Training page also says that during an internship you register for 16:198:844 Research Internship, and at the end of the semester you submit a report and a letter from the employer.

However, the Rutgers Global CPT page now says, following the latest SEVP guidance, that CPT can be authorized only for an internship that every student in the program must complete, or for off-campus work that thesis research genuinely requires. If you can finish the degree without that internship, Rutgers Global may not authorize CPT. The MSCS internship is not required, so under this rule it is unclear whether a regular summer internship can get CPT. This is the situation as of October 2026; check with the program office and ISSS before you enroll.

Another option is pre-completion OPT. According to the Rutgers Global page, it is limited to 20 hours a week while school is in session and can be full-time during breaks; it uses up part of your 12-month OPT allowance, with part-time time counted at half.

## Jobs and Location

Rutgers–New Brunswick is made up of the Busch, College Avenue, Douglass, Cook and Livingston campuses. The university website describes it as near the major urban centers of New York City and Philadelphia.

The MS program pages do not publish employment data, and csgrad has no job-search cases for this program yet.

## Application Requirements

These are the requirements on the official websites as of October 2026:

- Deadlines: for Fall 2027, February 1, 2027 for international applicants and March 1 for US citizens and green card holders; for Spring 2028, September 15, 2027 for international applicants. Admissions are not rolling: applications are reviewed once after each deadline, and applying early does not change your chances
- GRE: required. The department has no minimum, and the FAQ recommends Verbal 150 and Quant 160
- English: the Graduate School minimums are Writing 22, Speaking 23, Reading 21 and Listening 17 for TOEFL tests taken through January 21, 2026; Writing 4.5, Speaking 4.5, Reading 4 and Listening 4 for the new TOEFL taken from January 22, 2026 onward; IELTS 7 overall; Duolingo 120–130. If your entire degree was taught in English you can request a waiver, reviewed case by case
- Materials: 3 recommendation letters, a personal statement of about one page, a resume and transcripts from all previous schools; no application fee waivers
- Background: a CS bachelor's degree, or prerequisite coursework in calculus, linear algebra, discrete math, probability, numerical analysis, data structures, computer architecture and assembly, algorithms and so on. The department looks at your courses, not the name of your degree, and wants B+ or better in CS, math and related courses. Three-year bachelor's degrees are not accepted
- Deferring admission requires a $1,500 deposit, refunded when you enroll and kept if you do not

Two applicants submitted the TOEFL, 101–109; two submitted the GRE, 322–331. The admit had the highest GPA and test scores of the three; the reject has no test scores recorded; the waitlisted applicant had the lowest GPA and several research projects in China.
