# UCSC MSCSE

## Program Overview

The full degree name is Master of Science in Computer Science and Engineering, offered by the Computer Science and Engineering department in UC Santa Cruz's Baskin Engineering. There are two tracks: Thesis Plan I is for students who want to do research and may go on to a PhD, and Capstone Plan II is aimed at industry jobs.

- Campus: the main Santa Cruz campus, on the quarter system. UCSC's Silicon Valley campus in Santa Clara hosts the NLP MS, which is a separate professional master's program; the official CSE MS pages don't mention the Silicon Valley campus
- Credits and length (2026-27 catalog): 48 credits in total. The normative time is two years for the thesis track and one to two years for the capstone track, and the catalog notes that many students finish in four quarters; full-time students take at least two courses per quarter and finish within six quarters
- STEM: on the STEM OPT eligible majors list of the International Student and Scholar Services office (ISSP), CIP 11.0701
- Cost: the financial aid office's 2026-27 graduate budget (Fall, Winter and Spring quarters) lists $16,665 in tuition and fees plus $15,102 in nonresident supplemental tuition, $31,767 in total; campus health insurance is $7,455. With housing, food and other items, the total non-California-resident budget is $74,874. The CSE MS is not among the professional master's programs with separate fees (NLP, HCI and others) that the registrar lists
- Funding: the catalog states that the department does not guarantee MS students any financial support

## Courses and Registration

An alumnus tailored a thesis project to game design. If you have a research interest, look at potential advisers’ recent work and how research topics are chosen.

The 48 credits fall into four parts: 13 core, 15 breadth, 15 elective, and 5 for the thesis or capstone.

- Core: CSE 200 (Research and Teaching in CSE, 3 credits, completed in the first year), plus two of CSE 201 Analysis of Algorithms, CSE 210A Programming Languages and CSE 220 Computer Architecture
- Breadth: the department has 10 categories; take one course from each of 3 different categories. A course counts toward only one category and can't double-count as an elective; courses not on the department's list are assumed not to count
- Electives: these must be regular 5-credit graduate courses, and 2-credit seminars don't count. Up to 10 credits can come from other UCSC departments (courses outside Baskin Engineering need the graduate director's approval), and up to 5 credits from upper-division undergraduate CSE courses
- Completion: the thesis track writes a thesis plus 5 credits of CSE 299; the capstone track does a project plus 5 credits of CSE 297 or CSE 247 (Projects in Artificial Intelligence)

Courses counted toward the degree need a B- or better. Graduate courses from other schools can be petitioned as substitutes for equivalent UCSC courses, but they don't count toward the 48 credits, at most 3 can be transferred, and petitions are only considered after at least one quarter at UCSC.

The department lists these research areas: algorithms, logic and complexity; computer hardware; security and privacy; cyber-physical systems; distributed systems; databases; machine learning; NLP; networks; pervasive computing and human-computer interaction; programming languages; robotics; storage systems; and visual computing.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

An internship is not a degree requirement. The catalog says most MS students cover their costs by interning at Bay Area companies over the summer.

ISSP's CPT page (updated June 2026):

- You can apply only after one full academic year (3 quarters) of full-time enrollment
- If the internship isn't a degree requirement, you must enroll in a course during the CPT quarter; graduate students can use a research course
- During Fall, Winter and Spring, CPT is limited to 20 hours a week; continuing students can work full time over summer and breaks
- If summer is your final term, CPT is part-time only
- CPT is approved one quarter at a time; 12 or more months of full-time CPT makes you ineligible for OPT

So if you want a full-time internship in your first summer, you need at least one more quarter of study after it. After graduation, OPT can be extended under the STEM rules.

## Jobs and Location

The school is in Santa Cruz, and the department describes itself as close to Silicon Valley. Neither the department nor the graduate division publishes employment data for MS students.

## Application Requirements

- Deadline: January 26. The graduate division's deadline table was updated October 8, 2026; it doesn't name the entry year, but its "not accepting applications for 2027" notes indicate it covers 2027 entry
- GPA: minimum 3.0
- Background: applicants from any major are welcome. The department page says you need sufficient preparation in at least two of three areas, algorithms, computer architecture and programming languages, and it also lists three expected courses: data structures and algorithms, computer architecture, and programming languages (equivalent to UCSC's CSE 101, CSE 120 and CSE 114A). It also expects at least three more courses in related areas, such as operating systems, machine learning, networks or databases
- GRE: not required
- English: TOEFL iBT 4.5 on the new scale used from 2026 (CSE is in the higher-requirement group; other programs need 4.0), or 89 on the pre-2026 scale; IELTS overall 7. Scores are valid for two years. Applicants who finished a four-year bachelor's degree at an English-speaking institution can request a waiver, which is only granted after admission
- Materials: 3 letters of recommendation, a statement of purpose (1–2 pages single-spaced recommended), a CV and transcripts. The department page lists the personal history statement as required, while the graduate division page says it is optional for MS applicants, so go by what the application system asks for. The application fee is $155 for international applicants, and MS applicants aren't eligible for the department's fee waiver

## Admissions (csgrad DP)

csgrad has 3 self-reported UCSC MSCSE records, all for Fall entry:

- Fall 2023: 1 rejection
- Fall 2024: 1 admit
- Fall 2025: 1 admit

That's 2 admits and 1 rejection in total. All 3 applicants studied in mainland China (one each from a 985, a 211 and a non-985/211 school), 2 in CS and 1 in software engineering. The 2 GPAs on a 4-point scale range from 3.21 to 3.74, and the third is 92 on a 100-point scale; TOEFL 96–106 (2 records), IELTS 7.5 (1 record); the 2 who reported GRE scored 324–326 (GRE is no longer required). All 3 had research experience, and 2 had internships in China.

The rejected applicant actually had a higher GPA and class rank than the Fall 2024 admit. With only 3 records, there's no stable bar to read from this.
