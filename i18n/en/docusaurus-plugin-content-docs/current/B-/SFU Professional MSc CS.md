# SFU Professional MSc CS

## Program Overview

This is the professional master's offered by Simon Fraser University's School of Computing Science (part of the Faculty of Applied Sciences). The website now calls it the Master of Professional Computer Science (MPCS), while the co-op pages still use the name PMP. The three streams award different degrees: Big Data awards a Master of Science in Big Data, and the other two award a Master of Visual Computing and a Master of Cybersecurity. Only the Big Data stream is an MSc.

- Length: full-time, no thesis, at least 30 units. The website says 16–20 months; the normal length is 4 terms (16 months), including one four-month co-op.
- Intake: fall only. The school's FAQ says the professional master's has no spring intake.
- Campus: in Fall 2026, the Lab I course of each stream (CMPT 732/742/782) is scheduled at the Burnaby campus.
- Tuition (2026/27 entry): for international students, all three streams are CAD 13,856.16 per term, CAD 55,424.64 for four terms. The co-op fee is included in tuition; after four terms, a continuing fee of 50% is charged each term. Several entrance scholarships of up to CAD 15,000 each are offered every year.
- Canadian programs have no US-style STEM designation. For work after graduation, see PGWP below.

## Courses

All three streams share one structure: two 6-unit Lab courses, two required 3-unit courses, 9 units of graduate CMPT electives, plus 3 units of co-op or a project. Required courses in the Fall 2026 calendar:

- Big Data: CMPT 726 Machine Learning, CMPT 732/733 Big Data Lab I/II, CMPT 756 Distributed and Cloud Systems
- Visual Computing: CMPT 726, CMPT 742/743 Visual Computing Lab I/II, CMPT 756
- Cybersecurity: CMPT 756, CMPT 782/783 Cybersecurity Lab I/II, CMPT 789 Applied Cryptography

The usual sequence on the website: 9 units in each of the first two terms, then a four- or eight-month co-op, then a final 9-unit term back on campus.

Expected background listed on the admissions page: Java, Python and C++ (students planning graphics or vision courses may not need Java and C++ but do need Matlab); calculus, linear algebra and advanced statistics; data structures and algorithms, databases and operating systems.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

Canada has no CPT; internships go through the school's co-op program. The program pages describe a mandatory, paid co-op. The calendar is more specific: every student must apply, but placements are competitive. Before CMPT 626 Graduate Co-op I, you need 12 units of CMPT courses at the 700 level or above with a CGPA of at least 3.0. If you don't land a co-op for the summer term, no courses are offered that term; you can apply for a later co-op or take more courses. With the program director's approval, you can instead do CMPT 629 Graduate Project (3 units). One co-op term or two, it counts as 3 units either way.

Numbers from the school's co-op page: a 98% co-op placement rate for PMP students in 2016–2024, and an average monthly co-op salary of $4,685 in 2016–2023. A co-op can also be an on-campus research position, a project or specific coursework.

## Jobs and Location

Classes are in Burnaby, in Metro Vancouver. The program page cites the school's own MPCS alumni survey (2016–2024): 96% of graduates were employed within six months.

To stay and work in Canada after graduating, you go through the PGWP. Under current IRCC rules: since February 15, 2024, graduates of a master's program of at least 8 months can apply for a 3-year PGWP even if the program was shorter than two years; master's graduates have no field-of-study requirement; PGWP applications submitted on or after November 1, 2024 need CLB 7 in English in all four skills.

## Application Requirements

For the Fall 2027 cycle:

- Dates: applications open October 1, 2026, and close January 19, 2027. Offers go out on a rolling basis after the deadline; fall applicants usually hear by the end of April, and the school does not give status updates.
- Degree: a bachelor's in CS or a related field. Canadian degrees need a CGPA of 3.00/4.33, or 3.33/4.33 over the last 60 units; other countries follow the graduate studies equivalency table.
- Documents: 3 reference letters (professors, employers or mentors), a CV, and a statement of purpose of no more than two double-spaced pages. Upload unofficial transcripts first; official ones are needed only after you get an offer.
- GRE: the school's FAQ says it is not required.
- English: the program pages list no separate scores and follow the graduate studies requirements: IELTS 6.5 (6.0 in each section); TOEFL 4.5 (4.0 in each section) on the new scale, or 90 (20 in each section) for tests taken before January 21, 2026; Duolingo 125 (105 per section) and PTE 65 (60 per section) are also accepted. Scores are valid for two years.

## Admission Results (csgrad Data Points)

csgrad has 12 self-reported records for these three streams from 11 applicants:

- Fall 2023: Big Data 4 admits, 2 rejects; Cybersecurity 3 admits
- Fall 2024: Big Data 1 reject; Visual Computing 2 admits, both noted as applying to Big Data and being redirected to Visual Computing (one of them is that Big Data reject)
- No records for the 2025 or 2026 cycles

Backgrounds: 9 from mainland China universities (5 from 985 schools, and one each from Tsinghua/Peking, the "Huawu" top-five group, a 211 school and UCAS/ShanghaiTech/SUSTech), 1 from an overseas university, and 1 with no clear category. Majors: CS 6, software engineering 3, data science 1, information security 1. Admits had GPAs of 3.21–3.95 on a 4-point scale or 86.5–91.67 out of 100; TOEFL 96–109, plus 1 with IELTS 7.5; GRE 323–334 among those who sent it (quant 169–170), and 3 people were admitted without a GRE.

The two Big Data rejects in 2023 both had GPAs above 3.7 and ranked in the top 3%; one of them noted that Canadian programs shouldn't be treated as safeties and that you should apply early. Most data points are from 2023, so treat them as a rough reference only.
