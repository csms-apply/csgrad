# Duke MIDS

## Program Overview

This is Duke's data science master's. The program is called the Master in Interdisciplinary Data Science (MIDS), the degree requirements page lists the degree as an MS in Interdisciplinary Data Science, and you apply through the Duke Graduate School.

- Length: two years, 4 semesters, full-time, fall entry only; only the final semester can be part-time, and there is no online version
- Credits: 42, made up of 30 credits of IDS core courses and at least 12 elective credits
- Size: the MIDS class profiles say the Class of 2025 drew 873 applications, had a 16% acceptance rate and enrolled 53 students; the Class of 2024 figures are 905, 12% and 37
- Campus: Duke's main campus in Durham, North Carolina
- STEM: it is a STEM-designated program with CIP code 30.3001; the FAQ says international students can work in the US for up to 36 months after graduation
- Cost: in the Graduate School's 2026–27 master's cost table, tuition is $34,940 per semester; for international students, 9 months of tuition and fees come to $75,814 (including $4,290 for health insurance), and the estimated total with living costs is $101,780. The fourth semester can be part-time and billed per credit; the MIDS page gives the example that if you finish your electives early, the last semester costs about $14,000
- Funding: a limited number of partial tuition scholarships, based on need and merit, with no separate application

## Courses and Course Selection

Before you arrive you complete an online summer review (linear algebra, statistics, R and Python; you can test out module by module). There is also a required 3-week on-campus bootcamp before the semester starts, mainly covering Python, data structures and object-oriented programming, with no grades.

The sequence on the official courses page:

- Year 1 fall: IDS 702 Modeling and Representation of Data, IDS 706 Data Engineering Systems, and one of IDS 570 Text as Data or IDS 703 NLP; IDS 720 Practical Data Science is not required; a knowledge assessment near the end of bootcamp helps faculty advise whether you should take it, and about two-thirds of students take it each year
- Year 1 spring: IDS 705 Principles of Machine Learning, IDS 701, plus 1 elective
- Year 2 fall: IDS 704 on data ethics and policy, IDS 707 on data visualization
- All of year 2: IDS 798 Capstone, a project for a company, government agency or nonprofit, ending with a presentation and a white paper
- Every semester: the 0.5-credit Data Dialogues speaker series and MIDS Workshops (professional development)

Core courses cannot be waived based on undergraduate coursework, and transfer credits from other schools are not accepted.

You need at least 4 electives and 12 elective credits, each worth at least 2 credits, and they must be taken for a grade; Credit/No Credit and audited courses do not count. Relevant courses in math, statistics, ECE, CS, biostatistics and similar areas count without extra approval; the examples on the website include ECE 661, ECE 685D, ECE 551D and STA 602. Courses outside that list need a one-page memo to program staff. There is also a Quantitative Finance track run with the Math Department, which requires one finance career development course, at least two foundational quantitative finance courses and at least two applied ones, plus industry talks and seminars.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

A summer internship is a graduation requirement for all MIDS students. You do it between the first and second years, it has to relate to the MIDS curriculum, and you find it yourself with support from program staff. The website does not mention co-op.

F-1 students would normally use CPT. But the internship page currently says DHS has issued new CPT guidance to schools, Duke is evaluating it and has temporarily paused CPT for most (though not all) students, and you should ask Duke Visa Services about your case. The website does not say how the internship requirement is handled during the pause, so keep an eye on it before and after you apply.

## Jobs and Location

The school is in Durham, North Carolina. Past internship employers listed on the internship page include IBM, Adobe, Google, Facebook, Verily, Micron, WorldQuant, BlackRock, Bank of America and PwC, as well as research organizations such as RTI International. The website says many students get job offers after their internships.

## Application Requirements

- Deadline: Fall 2027 has a single round, January 28, 2027, with no early deadline; interviews are mostly in mid to late March and only some applicants are invited, and all decisions are out by March 31
- GRE: optional for the 2027 cycle, with no minimum GPA or test scores
- English: applicants whose first language is not English submit TOEFL, IELTS or Duolingo. The Graduate School generally looks for TOEFL iBT 90 (5 on the new TOEFL for tests taken on or after January 21, 2026), IELTS 7.0 and Duolingo 125; for Fall 2027, scores must be dated August 1, 2024 or later
- Recommendation letters: 3, academic references preferred
- Materials: transcripts, SOP and resume, plus a 3–5 minute video about a project you have worked on; no writing sample and no work experience required; the application fee is $105
- Background: any undergraduate major is accepted. The FAQ says successful applicants usually have quantitative coursework such as linear algebra and statistics, plus programming experience such as Python; applicants without quantitative coursework can show it through online courses, portfolios or code repositories
