# Umich ECE

## Program Overview

The University of Michigan's EECS department has two divisions, ECE and CSE. This program sits in ECE. The degree is the Master of Science (MS) or Master of Science in Engineering (MSE) in Electrical and Computer Engineering, and you apply through the Rackham Graduate School. The degree is not split into EE and CE; students are admitted into one of 13 major areas, and the program manual says it covers electrical engineering, systems science and computer engineering.

- The MS and MSE differ only in name; the requirements are the same. The website says students with an engineering bachelor's can choose either, and everyone else receives the MS (the Fall 2026 manual words it as an undergraduate electrical engineering degree)
- 30 credits, thesis optional. The website says students normally finish in 1–2 years; the manual's typical timeline is 3–4 semesters, with a 5-year limit
- Entry: mainly fall. The ECE application page also lists a Winter 2027 MS intake (deadline September 15, 2026), but the department FAQ says it accepts applications for the fall term only
- Campus: Ann Arbor; the program is intended for daytime students
- STEM: the department's pages do not list this degree's CIP code. The university's International Center says to check whether the CIP code in the Program of Study section on page 1 of your I-20 is on the DHS STEM list
- Cost: on the Registrar's 2026-27 tuition table, Rackham tuition in the College of Engineering for nonresidents is $33,467 per term at 9+ credits, plus $246.89 in mandatory fees and a $500 international student fee, about $34,214 per term
- Funding: the department does not offer master's students funding at admission. GSI and GSRA positions cover tuition and pay a stipend, but PhD students get priority, and the department says it is very rare for new students to get a GSI in their first term or year. MS students can later apply to the PhD program

## Courses and Course Selection

The 13 major areas: Applied Electromagnetics & RF Circuits, Computer Vision, Control Systems, Embedded Systems, Integrated Circuits & VLSI, MEMS & Microsystems, Network, Communication, and Information Systems, Optics & Photonics, Power & Energy, QuEST, Robotics, Signal & Image Processing and Machine Learning, and Solid State & Nanotechnology. To change areas after enrolling, you must first pass two courses in your admitted area and then get approval from the advisor for the new area.

Degree requirements in the Fall 2026 manual:

- 30 graduate credits, at least 24 of them in letter-graded technical courses
- At least 12 credits of ECE courses at the 500 level or above; at least 9 credits in your major area, 6 of them at the 500 level or above
- At most 6 credits of S/U-graded courses. A thesis (ECE 698) counts for up to 6 credits and lowers the technical course requirement to 21 credits
- A course needs a B- or better to count, and the GPA must stay at 3.0 or above; up to 6 graduate credits can be transferred from other schools
- A Plan of Study approved by your advisor is due within a month of the start of your first term

Technical courses are engineering, math, statistics, physics and similar courses; courses from economics, business or the School of Information need advisor approval. The department's course list marks which area each course counts toward. For example, Integrated Circuits & VLSI includes EECS 470 Computer Architecture, EECS 570 Parallel Computer Architecture and EECS 427 VLSI Design I; Embedded Systems includes EECS 473 Advanced Embedded Systems and ECE 505 Computational Data Science and Machine Learning; Signal & Image Processing and Machine Learning includes ECE 551 Matrix Methods and EECS 553 Machine Learning (ECE).

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

ECE's CPT rules for international students:

- CPT (ENGR 998) takes place in the spring/summer term, and students usually register for it in the preceding winter term
- You must enroll full time every fall and winter term, and CPT cannot replace full-time enrollment
- CPT is not allowed in your final term unless you still need to take courses required to finish the degree
- ENGR 998 is 1 credit, charged as usual, and does not count toward graduation. Before applying you attend a College of Engineering internship seminar and submit an offer letter, an advisor recommendation form and an essay, with a midterm and a final essay during the internship

## Jobs and Location

The university is in Ann Arbor, Michigan. The department holds career fairs just for ECE students, and internship, co-op and full-time job information for master's students is handled by the College of Engineering's ECRC. After graduation students use OPT, and you must be enrolled in courses in the term you apply for OPT.

## Application Requirements

- Deadline: January 15, 2027 for Fall 2027, with applications opening October 1, 2026; the department says applications received after the deadline are at a competitive disadvantage
- GRE: required if your bachelor's is from a non-US institution, not required for US undergraduates. The department looks for scores at the 50th percentile or higher in all three sections, and the FAQ says admitted students average at least 324
- 2025-26 MS admits averaged GPA 3.7, GRE Quant 168, Verbal 155 and Writing 3.6
- Background: a bachelor's in engineering, physics or mathematics, typically with a GPA of 3.5/4.0 or higher; if you already hold an ECE MS or PhD from another school, you cannot apply for the same degree
- English: the department FAQ lists TOEFL iBT 84 and IELTS 6.5, valid for two years; applicants whose bachelor's or master's comes from a school where instruction is entirely in English are exempt
- 3 recommendation letters, at least 2 from faculty or instructors who know your classroom or lab work
- Statements: an Academic Statement of Purpose (1–3 pages) and a Personal Statement (1 page)

## Admissions Data (csgrad DP)

csgrad currently has 7 data points for the UMich ECE master's (listed on the site as MSECE): 5 admits and 2 rejects, all for fall entry:

| Entry year | Admit | Reject |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 2 | 1 |
| 2024 | 3 | 0 |
| 2026 | 0 | 1 |

There is no data yet for 2025 or 2027.

All 5 admits studied in mainland China (1 from the "Huawu" top-five group, 2 from 985 schools, 2 from 211 schools), with majors in CS, software engineering, EE and other STEM fields. GPAs on a 4.0 scale were 3.70–3.96 (one more was 3.75 on a 4.3 scale), and those who reported a rank were in the top 3%–20%. GRE scores were 326–334 with Quant 169–170, and TOEFL 98–109. All 5 had 1–3 research experiences, 2 had research abroad, and most of their letters were research letters. The notes mention the Computer Vision area and the Network, Communication area (the latter with an MSE).

The 2 rejected applicants also studied in mainland China (985 CS and 211 other STEM), with percentage-scale GPAs of 89.3–90, GRE 322–332, and TOEFL 107 or IELTS 7.5; both had research and internship experience. The sample is too small to show a cutoff.
