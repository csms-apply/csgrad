# Wisc MSECE

## Program Overview

This is the master's in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering in UW–Madison's College of Engineering. The degree is the Master of Science in Electrical and Computer Engineering. You pick a named option when you apply, and three of them are taught in person on the Madison campus:

- Professional: course-only and accelerated, with a specialty area you choose
- Machine Learning and Signal Processing (MLSP): course-only and accelerated, focused on machine learning and signal processing
- Research: a traditional two-year program that ends in a thesis or project

There is also an online Power Engineering option for working professionals, which this page does not cover.

- Credits: at least 30 for every option, with at least 15 in graduate-level courses and a 3.00 graduate GPA
- Length: Professional and MLSP take 12–16 months, and the Guide requires completion within 3 academic semesters
- Entry: all three options admit for fall only
- STEM: the PDC admissions pages mark both Professional and MLSP as STEM OPT eligible
- Cost: Professional and MLSP are billed per credit; the PDC pages list $1,200 per credit, so 30 credits come to $36,000 in tuition alone. For 2026-27, segregated fees add $849.74 per semester at 8 or more credits. Research uses standard graduate tuition; check the Bursar's rate tables for the amount
- Funding: Professional and MLSP students are not eligible for the tuition remission that comes with assistantships, and the department strongly discourages them from taking TA/RA/PA positions. Research applicants are automatically considered for department TA funding, but funding is limited and not guaranteed, and the department tells admitted students to plan to fund themselves

The department handbook describes Professional and MLSP as terminal degrees, not meant for students who plan to go on to a PhD.

## Courses and Course Selection

All three options require the 1-credit ECE 610 seminar in the first fall. The maximum load is 15 credits per term.

Professional: at least 20 credits of ECE courses numbered 400 or above, with 12 credits from one curriculum path. The other 9 credits can be courses from the paths, up to 2 credits of ECE 702 internship, up to 3 credits of independent study, or advisor-approved courses numbered 300 or above in other departments (up to 9 credits). The paths are Computer Engineering (split into Networking and Security; CAD for Digital Electronic Systems; Computer Architecture and Embedded Systems), Electromagnetic Fields and Waves, Energy and Power Systems, and Solid State and Photonics; with advisor approval you can also design your own. In the department's 2026-27 handbook, the Computer Architecture course list includes CS 537 Operating Systems, ECE/CS 552, ECE/CS 752 and ECE 757 Advanced Computer Architecture, ECE 751 Embedded Computing Systems and ECE/CS 755 VLSI; the Networking and Security list includes CS 642, ECE/CS 707 and ECE/CS 782, among others.

MLSP: at least one machine learning course (ECE/CS/ME 532 or 539; ECE/CS 561, 760 or 761; ECE/CS/STAT 861), at least one signal processing course (ECE 431, ECE/CS 533, ECE 734, 735 or 738), and at least 12 more credits from the MLSP course list. The remaining 11 credits are chosen with your advisor and can be an internship, a project, or courses numbered 300 or above in other departments (up to 9 credits, typically CS, math, statistics or EPD). The Guide says successful students have some background in linear algebra, statistics and programming.

Research: at least 15 credits at the 700 level or above, at least 15 credits of ECE courses numbered 400 or above, at least 3 credits of ECE 790 Master's Research, and a final thesis or project.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

- Neither Professional nor MLSP requires an internship. The handbook strongly encourages Professional students to do one professional development activity (an ECE 702 internship, InterEGR 601, independent study or EPD courses), and highly recommends an internship or hands-on project for MLSP. Its sample plan is 10–13 credits in the first fall, 9–12 in spring, an internship or a few credits over the summer, and the remaining credits in the second fall
- The ISS CPT page says that effective August 24, 2026, ISS can only approve CPT that is required by the student's program; CPT approved before that date stays valid through its end date. On October 5, a group of higher education organizations sued over the change, and ISS is monitoring it. Internships are not required in either course-based option, so whether a summer internship after Fall 2027 entry can use CPT depends on the ISS rules at that time. ISS also says pre-completion OPT may be an option when a program does not require practical training
- 365 or more days of full-time CPT makes you ineligible for post-completion OPT at the same degree level

## Jobs and Location

The school is in Madison, Wisconsin. The College of Engineering's Engineering Career Services coordinates co-ops and internships, career fairs and interviews and gives job-search advice; the university posts openings on Handshake. The department does not publish employment rates or outcomes for this master's, and csgrad has no job-search cases for it yet.

## Application Requirements

- Deadlines: Professional and MLSP close on February 15; the PDC pages list February 15, 2027 for Fall 2027, and after that applications are considered through April 15 only if space remains. Research closes on December 1, which means December 1, 2026 for Fall 2027
- GRE: not required for any option; Professional and MLSP may consider it if submitted
- GPA: the Graduate School requires 3.00 on the last 60 semester credits, reported on your school's own scale; do not convert it to a 4.0 scale yourself
- English: TOEFL 92 (tests taken before January 21, 2026) or 5.0 on the new scale, IELTS 7.0, Duolingo 125, and scores must be less than two years old on the first day of the entry term. TOEFL 80–91 (4.5 on the new scale), IELTS 6.5 or Duolingo 115–124 may lead to conditional admission, which means taking the ESLAT on arrival and completing any required English course in the first semester
- Three letters of recommendation, plus a statement of purpose, résumé/CV and unofficial transcripts. Research applicants also list a research interest and a proposed faculty advisor and submit a brief video
- The application fee is $75, plus $6 for international applicants; one fee covers up to three programs for the same entry term
