# Umich MSDS

## Program Overview

This is the University of Michigan's data science master's. The degree is the Master of Science in Data Science (the website also calls it MDS). You apply through the Rackham Graduate School, and the application page and program office are on the LSA Statistics department's website. Statistics courses come from Statistics and Biostatistics, and computing courses come from CSE and the School of Information. The website says every student trains in both areas, and there are no tracks.

- Length: the Statistics FAQ says most students finish in 2 years (4 semesters)
- Credits: the program guide does not give a fixed credit total. It requires at least 25 graduate credits taken while in the program, 18 of them at the advanced graduate level (500-level or above in LSA and Engineering, 600-level or above in the School of Information and the School of Public Health)
- Entry: fall only
- Campus: Ann Arbor, in person; part-time study is typically not available to F-1 international students
- STEM: the FAQ says the program is recognized as STEM, so graduates may be eligible for the STEM OPT extension
- Cost: the website says the cost is the student's responsibility; tuition depends on how many credits you register for each term, and international students pay the out-of-state rate. In the university's FY2027 tuition table, LSA graduate tuition and required fees for fall 2026 are $15,900 per term in-state and $31,802 per term out-of-state, and students on F or J visas pay an extra $500 international fee each term
- There is no thesis option; the FAQ says the master's programs use R, Python and C++

## Courses and Course Selection

The curriculum offers flexibility, though courses in other departments can involve forms and waitlists. Students pursuing SDE roles may need additional preparation alongside the DS and MLE coursework.

Under the program guide revised in November 2025, the curriculum has five parts:

- Pre-core (17–19 credits): MATH/DATASCI 403 Discrete Mathematics and EECS 402 Programming (taken in the first fall semester), EECS 403 Data Structures and Algorithms, plus one probability course and one statistical inference course (STATS or BIOSTAT options)
- Data management (7–8 credits): one database course (EECS 484 or CSE 584), plus one of EECS 485/486, CSE 549/SI 650, SI 618 or DATASCI 507. EECS 485 Web Systems is open to MDS students in the Spring term only
- Data science techniques (6–8 credits): one regression course (BIOSTAT 650, STATS 413 or STATS 500) and one machine learning or data mining course (EECS 545, EECS 553, EECS 476, SI 670, DATASCI 503 and others)
- Capstone (3–4 credits): STATS 504, SI 699 Big Data Analytics, BIOSTAT 699, or an approved directed study
- Electives (at least 9 credits): one course each from Principles of Data Science, Data Analysis and Computation, with at least two advanced graduate courses. The Computation category includes EECS 481 Software Engineering, CSE 586 Algorithms, CSE 587 Parallel Computing, CSE 592 AI, CSE 595 NLP and others

If you have taken equivalent courses with a B- or better, you can request waivers after admission and before the program starts, but you still need at least 25 credits inside the program. Each course can satisfy only one requirement, and you need a GPA of 3.0 or above to graduate. The FAQ recommends taking no more than 3–4 courses a semester.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

The program does not require an internship. The FAQ says many students do one, but the department does not guarantee internships or jobs. On the International Center's CPT course list (July 2026 version), this program is not among the programs with a required internship; F-1 students doing an optional internship must enroll in a CPT course, and the list says all Rackham graduate students can use RACKHAM 998.

## Jobs and Location

The website does not publish employment rates or salaries for this program. The FAQ lists government, insurance, banking and finance, medical research, pharmaceuticals and tech as sectors graduates go into, with sample titles such as Data Scientist, Data Analyst, Machine Learning Engineer, Software Development Engineer and Product Scientist. Some graduates go on to PhDs in statistics, biostatistics or economics. The FAQ says the department does not offer a data science PhD.

## Application Requirements

- Deadline: the website says January 15 at 11:59 PM Pacific time; there is no priority deadline and no rolling review. Under this rule, Fall 2027 applications are due January 15, 2027. Decisions usually come out in March or April
- Prerequisites: 2 semesters of calculus, 1 semester of linear algebra and 1 introductory computing course, from an institution that issues official transcripts; MOOCs do not count. The application asks for prerequisite course information and a syllabus for each course
- Background: a data science bachelor's is not required; most admitted students come from data science, CS, information, engineering, physics, statistics or mathematics
- GPA: no minimum; review is holistic, and grades in relevant courses count more than overall GPA
- GRE: not required and optional to submit; the website says scores play only a minor, supporting role
- English: TOEFL iBT minimum 84; scores below the minimum are rejected automatically and the department cannot waive this. IELTS, ECPE and MET are also accepted, Duolingo is not. Applicants who completed their entire undergraduate degree at an English-only institution are exempt
- 2 recommendation letters, with an optional third; the FAQ says most should be academic
- Statements: a Statement of Purpose (no stated length limit), a Personal Statement (500 words maximum, and different from the SoP) and a resume
- Application fee: $90 for international students, who are not eligible for fee waivers

The Statistics FAQ gives combined numbers for its master's programs: in recent years it has made well over 100 offers a year, about 70 students enroll, and about 80% are international. It also says each program receives thousands of applications for fewer than 100 places.

## Admissions Data (csgrad DP)

csgrad currently has 5 data points for the UMich data science master's (listed on the site as DS): 3 admits and 2 rejects, all for fall entry:

| Entry year | Admit | Reject |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 2 | 0 |
| 2024 | 1 | 1 |
| 2025 | 0 | 1 |

There is no data yet for 2026 or 2027.

All 3 admits studied in mainland China (one each from the "Huawu" top-five group, a 985 school and a 211 school), majoring in AI, information systems and a CS-related interdisciplinary field. GPAs on a 4.0 scale were 3.82–3.87 (the third was 87 on a 100-point scale); GRE scores were 322–327, with Quant 168–170 among those who reported section scores; TOEFL 105–109. The two who listed their experience each had 3 research projects and 1 internship, and their letters included research, course and internship letters. Their reported experience includes a data analyst internship, a research institute internship, and interdisciplinary areas such as medical imaging and bioinformatics. One of the admits ultimately enrolled in this program.

The 2 rejected applicants also studied in mainland China (Huawu CS, and DS at UCAS/ShanghaiTech/SUSTech), one with 3.87 on a 4.0 scale and one with 87.57 on a 100-point scale; GRE 321–326, TOEFL 101–107, and both had research experience. One rejected applicant's GPA and GRE were no lower than the admits', and the sample is too small to show a cutoff. All 5 submitted GRE scores.
