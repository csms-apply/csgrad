# UW MSECE

## Program Overview

This is the daytime full-time master's in the ECE department at the University of Washington (Seattle). The department calls it the Daytime Master's Program, and the degree is the Master of Science in Electrical and Computer Engineering (MSECE). The degree used to be the MSEE: the department's graduate page now lists only the MSECE, the old requirements-msee link opens the MSECE degree requirements, and only the degree audit worksheet's file name still says MSEE. The evening [UW EE PMP](../A/uw%20ee%20pmp.md) is a separate program in the same department.

- Credits: 42, with a coursework option and a thesis option. The department says coursework is usually chosen by students headed for industry, and the thesis option is meant for students who want a PhD
- Length: the FAQ says the program generally takes 1.5–2 years. It cannot be done part-time; the department points people who want part-time study to the PMP
- Entry: once a year, in autumn quarter only
- Campus: Seattle
- STEM: the department's pages do not give this degree's CIP code. ISS says STEM OPT requires a degree in a field on the STEM list; you can confirm with ISS after you enroll
- Cost: the department's tuition page gives no amount and points to UW's graduate tuition dashboard, where you look up rates by program and residency
- Funding: the department says most master's students are self-funded. MSECE students can apply for a limited number of TA and RA positions, offered quarterly in ECE and in other departments

## Courses and Course Selection

Both options require 42 credits, at least 30 of them at the 500 level or above, and both require the 1-credit EE 500 seminar.

Coursework option:

- At least 24 credits of numerically graded EE 5xx courses (seminars and EE 599 do not count)
- At most 8 credits of EE 599 research
- At most 4 credits of seminars, including at most 2 credits of non-EE seminars
- At most 10 credits of non-EE courses, including at most 4 credits at the 300 level and at most 4 credits of ENGR 601

Thesis option: at least 20 credits of numerically graded EE 5xx courses, 13–17 research credits (4–8 of EE 599 and 9–13 of EE 700), and a written thesis followed by a final oral exam. The caps on non-EE courses and seminars are the same as in the coursework option.

The department says coursework students can design their own specialization. The curriculum groups on the graduate page are Biosystems, Communications and Networking, Data Science, Electronic, Photonic, and Integrated Quantum Systems (EPIQS), Power and Energy Systems, and Robotics and Controls. There is also a Graduate Data Science Option.

An EE bachelor's is not required. The flyer recommends UW's EE 215, EE 233 and EE 241/242 as prerequisites, plus programming at the level of CSE 121–123, and the admissions page says applicants are expected to make up any gaps in their ECE background.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

- ENGR 601 is the College of Engineering's internship course. Internship credit for graduate students must be approved by the Engineering Career Center, and the course is credit/no-credit only. At most 4 credits count toward the MSECE, and it is not required
- ISS rules for CPT: you generally need to finish one year (three quarters) of full-time study in the U.S. first; you must register for at least 1 CPT credit in each CPT quarter; part-time CPT allows up to 20 hours a week; 12 months or more of full-time CPT makes you ineligible for OPT
- The ISS CPT page now says that for requests submitted from September 18 on, it only approves internships that are required of all students in the program, and that where a program lets students choose between an internship and a non-internship option, international students should choose the non-internship option. The MSECE internship is not required, so if you want to intern on CPT during the program, check with the department and ISS before you enroll
- The MSECE pages say nothing about co-ops

## Jobs and Location

The school is in Seattle. The flyer says master's graduates have gone to research institutions and to companies such as Microsoft, Apple, Boeing, Facebook, Google and Amazon. The department does not publish an employment rate or salaries for this degree.

## Application Requirements

- Deadline: the department says applications open October 1 each year and close December 15, and materials that arrive after the deadline are not reviewed. On that schedule, Autumn 2027 applicants need everything in by December 15, 2026
- GRE: not required or accepted for the MS or PhD
- GPA: a cumulative 3.0/4.0 over the last 90 graded quarter credits (or 60 graded semester credits). The FAQ says there is no rigid GPA cutoff and the whole application is evaluated
- English: under Graduate School Policy 3.2, the minimums are TOEFL iBT 4.0 (80 for tests taken before January 21, 2026), IELTS 6.5 and Duolingo 115. You need TOEFL 4.5 (92 on the old scale), IELTS 7.0 or Duolingo 130 to avoid required English courses after you enroll, and the department recommends reaching that level. Scores are valid for two years
- 3 recommendation letters; the department recommends a statement of purpose of no more than three double-spaced pages; you also submit a CV and scanned transcripts, and the application fee is $90
- Flyer figures: in 2025-26, the MSECE had 796 applicants and made 129 offers

## Comparison with Similar Programs

- [UW EE PMP](../A/uw%20ee%20pmp.md): the same department's evening master's, taught in person in the evenings. It can be done full-time or part-time, and it is coursework-focused with no research or thesis requirement. The department says the daytime program fits better if you want research, a thesis, a PhD, or a fully funded research position
- [Gatech ECE](../A-/Gatech%20ECE.md), [Duke ECE](../A-/duke%20ece.md), [CMU ECE](../A/CMU%20ECE.md): other ECE master's programs to compare on credits and internship rules
