# Cornell ECE MEng

## Program Overview

The degree is the Master of Engineering (M.Eng.) in Electrical and Computer Engineering, offered by the ECE department in Cornell's Duffield College of Engineering. This page covers the Ithaca campus. The department also runs an ECE MEng at Cornell Tech in New York City, which it describes as focused on digital technology and entrepreneurship; courses offered only at Cornell Tech are not open to Ithaca students.

- 30 credits. The department says it can be finished in one year, and students may voluntarily extend to a third semester
- Admits for both fall and spring
- STEM: the engineering college's MEng FAQ says the MEng degree makes you eligible for STEM OPT
- Cost: in the Bursar's 2026-27 table, the MEng is billed at the Tier 1 rate, $36,973 per semester or $73,946 per year, plus a graduate student activity fee of $101 per year
- The program is self-funded, and aid is limited and purely merit-based. The college's Dean's Fellowship covers full tuition for two semesters and needs no separate application, but preference goes to domestic applicants; you need a complete application in by February 1 for full consideration

## Courses and Course Selection

- ECE 5010 Professional Seminar (S/U) is required in the first semester
- Every full-time semester you must take at least 2 ECE courses of 3 or more credits each; the design project, seminars and independent study do not count toward these 2
- Design project: ECE 5995 (fall) and ECE 5996 (spring), 3–8 credits in total and usually 1–4 per semester. It must span two semesters, and you must register for at least 1 credit of it in your first semester
- Technical electives: ECE courses at the 5000 level or above, 5000+ courses from any Duffield engineering field (CS, MSE and ORIE all count), and a few courses on the Johnson business school's approved list. Undergraduate courses at the 4000 level or below never count. The department suggests 2–4 electives, 6–16 credits in total
- Every course needs a C or better. At most 2 S/U credits count, so besides ECE 5010 you can take only one more S/U course

The department lists its strategic focus areas as Bio-Electrical Engineering; Circuits and Electronic Systems; Computer Engineering; Information, Networks, and Decision Systems; Physical Electronics, Devices, and Plasma Science; and Robotics and Autonomy.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

- An internship is not a degree requirement, and the school does not place you; you find it yourself
- Only students who voluntarily add a third semester can intern, during the summer between the second and third semesters
- International students in paid internships must apply for CPT and, in the semester right after the internship, enroll in the 1-credit ECE 5999 (letter grade) and submit an 8–10 page paper graded by the MEng director
- Taking a leave in the third semester for a longer internship is, according to the department, very unlikely to work because of immigration rules; and without a legitimate academic reason, I-20s are not extended to a fourth semester

The official pages do not mention co-op.

## Jobs and Location

The ECE department's MEng overview page (shared by the Ithaca and Cornell Tech programs) has a Featured Employers section including Apple, Amazon, Meta, AMD, NVIDIA, Intel, Google, Qualcomm, Microsoft, TikTok and Cadence, and a Job Title Examples list including ASIC Design Engineer, Design Verification Engineer, Embedded Software Engineer, Firmware Engineer and Software Engineer. The page does not say whether these are where graduates went, and gives no year, headcount or placement rate.

## Application Requirements

- Deadlines: the department's Ithaca admissions page lists January 17 for fall and October 15 for spring, with rolling review. The page does not name a cycle, so check the date in the application system again before submitting for Fall 2027
- GRE not required; application fee $105
- Recommended GPA of 3.5/4.0 or higher
- 2 recommendation letters, faculty letters strongly suggested
- Statements: a one-page Academic Statement of Purpose and a Personal Statement; résumé optional but encouraged
- Prerequisites: an ECE undergraduate degree is the best preparation. You should have circuits, digital logic and computer organization, calculus (including linear algebra and vector calculus), probability and statistics, intro programming plus an intermediate C/C++/Python course; depending on your direction, intro courses in VLSI, power systems, alternative energy or AI may also be expected
- English: only TOEFL iBT and IELTS Academic are accepted. The engineering college's MEng application guide lists IELTS overall 7.0, and for TOEFL only section minimums: on the new scale Speaking 4.5, Reading 4.5, Listening 5.0, Writing 5.0; on the former scale 23, 22, 22, 24. The guide calls these college-wide minimums that individual programs may raise, and does not say which year they apply to. The Graduate School's general minimums are lower, so prepare to the engineering college's standard for this program

## Admissions Results (csgrad DP)

csgrad currently has 6 data points, 3 admits and 3 rejects, all for fall entry:

| Entry year | Admit | Reject |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 0 | 1 |
| 2023 | 2 | 2 |
| 2024 | 1 | 0 |

There is no data yet for 2025 through 2027.

All 6 applicants studied at 985 universities in mainland China. Of the 3 admits, 2 majored in EE and 1 in CS: the two EE applicants had 4.0-scale GPAs of 3.96–3.98, and the CS applicant had about 89/100 (top 30%, with an exchange semester). TOEFL ranged from 101 to 115, and only one admit submitted a GRE (326). All 3 had research experience, 2 of them overseas, and most letters were research letters. The CS admit noted being admitted without a hardware background.

The 3 rejected applicants all majored in software engineering: GPA 3.85/4.0, or 90–92/100 (those who reported rank were in the top 3%); TOEFL 100–103 or IELTS 7; one submitted a GRE (325). They also all had research experience. One of them commented that the program seems to prefer a hardware background and hardware-heavy coursework.

The sample is very small. It only shows that EE applicants have a better record here than software engineering applicants; no admissions cutoff can be inferred.
