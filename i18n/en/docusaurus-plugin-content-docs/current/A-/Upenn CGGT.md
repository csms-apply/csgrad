# Upenn CGGT

## Program Overview

The Master of Science in Engineering in Computer Graphics and Game Technology is offered by the CIS department at Penn Engineering and was established in 2004. The program page says it prepares students for roles that need both art and engineering skills, such as designers, technical animators, technical directors and game programmers. Students can use the motion capture studio, NVIDIA GPUs and VR/AR equipment at the SIG Center for Computer Graphics.

- Length: 9 courses plus a one-semester design project, 10 courses in total; the program site says three to four semesters
- Start term and campus: the application page only lists fall entry (Fall 2027); main campus in Philadelphia
- STEM: neither the program page nor the degree requirements page mentions a STEM designation or CIP code; ask the program if it matters to you
- Cost (2026-27 academic year): on-campus master's students are charged by the number of CUs they register for, with tuition of $8,825 per CU. In the cost table, 3 CU comes to $29,399 including fees and 4 CU to $38,224. The living-cost budget for fall and spring of the same year is $28,484, including $5,018 for health insurance
- Funding: Penn Engineering generally does not fund master's students; you can look for a TA or part-time lab position in the department yourself

## Courses and Course Selection

The graphics curriculum builds foundations and can be demanding. Students targeting general SDE roles should also make time for algorithms and interview preparation.

Four courses are fixed requirements: DSGN 5005 3-D Computer Modeling, CIS 5600 Interactive Computer Graphics, CIS 5620 Computer Animation, and CIS 6600 Advanced Topics in Computer Graphics and Animation.

The other six are chosen by category:

- 1 graphics elective, for example CIS 5650 GPU Programming, CIS 5660 Procedural Graphics or CIS 5610 Advanced Computer Graphics; FNAR video and animation courses also count
- 1 math-based course, for example CIS 5810 Computer Vision, CIS 5150 Fundamentals of Linear Algebra & Optimization or ENM 5020 Numerical Methods & Modeling
- 1 AI/ML course, for example CIS 5190, CIS 5200, CIS 5210, or EAS 5870 Generative AI for Virtual Environments
- 1 technical elective: any 5000-level or higher course in Penn Engineering
- 1 free elective: any 5000-level or higher course at Penn related to graphics, animation or games, with the program director's approval
- 1 project course: CIS 5680 Game Design Practicum, or a master's thesis (CIS 9990, which also requires CIS 5990 Independent Study)

Students who took substantially similar courses as undergraduates can petition for substitutions, approved case by case by the program director.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

The degree requirements do not include an internship or co-op. Penn ISSS's CPT page (updated October 2, 2026) says SEVP issued new guidance on August 24, 2026: academic credit alone is not enough, and the training must be a required part of the program's established curriculum. Since September 10, Penn has resumed processing only requests that meet this standard, the employer must also sign Penn's cooperative agreement, and programs are still under review. So whether a CGGT student's summer internship can go through CPT is uncertain right now; confirm with the program and ISSS.

## Jobs and Location

Penn is in Philadelphia. The program page says graduates of CGGT and the undergraduate DMD program have gone to companies such as Pixar, DreamWorks, Disney, EA, Microsoft, Sony and Activision. It does not publish employment rates or salaries.

## Application Requirements

- Deadlines (Fall 2027): applications open September 15, 2026; the early deadline is November 1, 2026 (a footnote lists CGGT among the programs it applies to) and the regular deadline is February 1, 2027, both at 11:59 p.m. Eastern. Applications are reviewed on a rolling basis and all decisions are released by April 1. The CGGT entry in the master's programs list shows outdated years, so go by the How to Apply page
- Background: the program assumes a bachelor's in CS or engineering; the listed prerequisite is a bachelor's in CS, mechanical or electrical engineering, digital media and technology, or game design. Applicants with at least two years of CS/engineering coursework at a four-year university are also considered
- Non-CS/engineering backgrounds: the CGGT page says these students first complete a year of MCIT and then CGGT, taking 16 courses and earning two degrees. However, MCIT stopped accepting applications as of Fall 2026 and has been replaced by MAS-CS, so ask the program how this path works now
- GRE: optional this cycle
- English: recommended scores are TOEFL 5.0 on the new scale (100 on the legacy scale), IELTS 7.5 and DET 135; these are not hard cutoffs. The requirement is waived if you graduate from a U.S. program, attend school in a country where English is an official language, or graduate from an English-medium institution and your transcript says so
- Materials: 2 recommendation letters (at least 1 from a faculty member familiar with your academic ability), a personal statement of two pages or less, a resume and transcripts; the application fee is $95

All 4 admits studied at universities in mainland China: two from 985 schools, one from the "Huawu" top-five group and one from a Sino-foreign joint university. Three majored in CS and one in an interdisciplinary CS field with a design background. GPAs on a 4.0 scale were 3.78–3.84, and one applicant had 90 on a 100-point scale; class ranks ranged from the top 5% to below the top 50%. Only 1 submitted a GRE (332). TOEFL scores were 102–107, and one applicant had IELTS 7. All 4 had research experience and top-venue papers, published or under submission (3 as first author); the two who named their venues mostly listed vision and 3D vision conferences. 3 had internships in China, and each had at least one research recommendation letter.

The one reject applied for fall 2022 entry from a "Huawu" top-five school, majoring in CS, with about 89 on a 100-point scale, GRE 330, TOEFL 102 and two research experiences, and listed no papers. The sample is too small to infer an admissions bar.
