# Purdue MSECE

## Program Overview

This is the master's in Purdue's Elmore Family School of Electrical and Computer Engineering. The degree is the Master of Science in Electrical and Computer Engineering (MSECE). The school says all MSECE students earn the same degree, but most master's students are admitted to its Professional Master's Program (PMP). Thesis and course-only options also exist, and which track you are admitted to depends on available space.

- Campus: West Lafayette or Purdue in Indianapolis. Admissions criteria are the same for both, and students are placed by space availability; Indianapolis students can take a shuttle to attend classes in West Lafayette
- Credits: 30, of which at least 15 must be Purdue technical ECE graduate credits
- Length: the project-track page says most students take 4 semesters, and you can finish faster
- Entry: Fall and Spring; no Summer entry
- STEM: the ECE pages do not list a CIP code. According to Purdue ISS, STEM OPT eligibility depends on whether the CIP code on page 1 of your I-20 is on the DHS STEM list
- Cost: in the Bursar's 2026-27 professional master's fee table, the ECE: Technology Innovation line charges international students $15,214 per Fall/Spring semester, plus a $500-per-semester international fee, for $31,428 per academic year. The university estimates another $21,284 for housing, food, books, transportation and miscellaneous costs
- Funding: the school says PMP students are not eligible for funding, but they may move to the thesis or course-only option and accept funding under the conditions in their admission letter

## Courses and Course Selection

ECE is organized into 7 areas, each with one core course. For example, Computer Engineering (CE) uses ECE 60800 Algorithms and Complexity, and VLSI uses ECE 55900 MOS VLSI Design.

PMP students starting in Fall 2025 or later choose one of two tracks:

- Project track (Concepts to Innovation): West Lafayette only. 1 core course and 1 math course; you take ECE 60001/60002 over two consecutive semesters, Fall then Spring, and build a design project in a team that mixes technical areas; plus a 1-credit communication course and 2–3 credits of professional development (intellectual property, project management, engineering economics and similar)
- Implementation track: offered at both locations. 2 core courses and 2 math courses, plus at least 9 credits of implementation/professional development coursework, including the 1-credit ECE 60004

The implementation-track course list has a fair number of software-leaning courses, such as Machine Learning, Advanced Software Engineering, Computer Architecture, Embedded Systems, Compilers, Deep Learning, Datacenter and Cloud Networks, Intro to Operating Systems and Computer Vision, along with VLSI, power and communications courses. The master's handbook says related-area courses can include qualified courses from other Purdue schools, but they have to go on your plan of study for your advisory committee to review.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

The ECE pages state that the degree does not require an internship. If you want one:

- Keep at least a 3.0 GPA and make satisfactory progress toward the degree
- Internships are allowed after two completed semesters; a co-op can start after one
- A co-op needs at least two work terms with the same company, at least one of them in Fall or Spring; Fall/Spring terms run 16–17 weeks and Summer about 12 weeks, all full-time
- Part-time positions under 32 hours a week are not approved, and CPT is not allowed in your final semester
- You register for a zero-credit, no-tuition ENGR course through the Office of Professional Practice to keep full-time status; review takes about 3 weeks

## Jobs and Location

The main campus is in West Lafayette, Indiana. ECE runs its own Boilermaker Circuit career fair every semester, one in West Lafayette and one in Indianapolis (September 17 and 18 in Fall 2026), and ECE students can attend either or both.

ECE's admissions help guide says the West Lafayette campus does not offer on-campus housing for graduate students and housing is limited, so look for an off-campus place early.

## Application Requirements

- Deadlines: for Fall, December 1 is the priority deadline (also the priority date for funding) and April 1 is the final deadline; Spring is September 1. On this schedule, Fall 2027 maps to December 1, 2026 and April 1, 2027. The graduate school page says only a limited number of admissions go to applications completed after December 1
- Decisions: applications received by December 1 get final decisions by May 15; those received by April 1, by July 15
- GPA: the graduate school's page for ECE lists a 3.25/4.0 minimum for master's applicants. ECE's benchmark is an average of 3.60 or higher for US applicants, and typically the top 5% of the class for international applicants
- GRE: optional. The school says applicants with a lower GPA or from a school ECE may not know well may benefit from strong GRE scores
- English: you must meet the graduate school minimums, and ECE adds a writing requirement. TOEFL taken before January 21, 2026: 80 overall and 22 in writing; the new TOEFL taken on or after that date: 4 overall and 4.5 in writing; IELTS: 6.5 overall and 6 in writing. The ECE pages only list TOEFL and IELTS
- Recommendation letters: 3, and review starts only after all three arrive
- Materials: transcripts, Academic Statement of Purpose, Personal History Statement, resume; the application fee is $75 for international applicants
- Background: applicants typically have three semesters of calculus, differential equations and linear algebra. Students with a bachelor's in another science or engineering field can enroll, but they have to pick up the prerequisites for their graduate courses on their own
