# CMU MSIS

## Program Overview

MSIS is the Master of Science in Information Security, offered by the Information Networking Institute (INI) in CMU's College of Engineering. It was founded in 2003. INI also runs MSIN, MSAIE-IS, MSIT-IS and MSMITE.

- Length: Standard is three semesters and 123 units. Advanced is four semesters and 159 units; the extra 36 units go to a thesis, a development-based project or an Area of Concentration. Starting Fall 2027, INI adds Applied (three semesters, 126 units) and Applied Advanced (four semesters, 162 units), where the extra 3 units are a required internship. You pick three or four semesters in the application.
- Start terms: the INI admissions FAQ says it also accepts applications for spring entry, but the admissions page currently lists deadlines only for Fall 2027.
- Location: Pittsburgh. All new INI students start in Pittsburgh; only the Bicoastal programs, MSIT-IS and MSMITE, move to Silicon Valley after two semesters, and MSIS is not one of them. MSIS also has a part-time option for working professionals in the Pittsburgh area; its Standard option is also 123 units and can be finished in 2 to 6 years.
- STEM: the program page, curriculum page and admissions FAQ do not mention a STEM designation. Ask INI directly if you need to confirm it.
- Tuition: for 2026-27, tuition is $30,755 per semester, or $855 per unit in a semester with fewer than 36 units. At that rate, three semesters come to $92,265 in tuition and four semesters to $123,020, not counting a summer internship course. The site says tuition is usually set each spring for the next year and will likely go up.

## Courses and Course Selection

Students come from varied backgrounds and job searching is an active part of the experience. Note that this program is distinct from Heinz’s MISM.

For students entering in 2026-27 (the MS38 cohort), the requirement is 75 core units plus 48 elective units:

- Systems core, 12 units, choose one of five: 14-642 Introduction to Embedded Systems, 14-736 Distributed Systems: Techniques, Infrastructure, and Services, 15-605 Operating System Design and Implementation, 15-640 Distributed Systems, 15-746 Storage Systems
- Networking core, 12 units, choose one of four: 14-740, 14-760, 15-641, 18-756
- Security core, 24 units: 14-741 Introduction to Information Security is required, plus one of 14-742, 14-735 Secure Coding, 14-828 Browser Security, 18-731 Network Security, 18-732 Secure Software Systems, 18-733 Applied Cryptography, 18-734/17-731 Foundation of Privacy
- Business & Management core: 14-817 Cyber Risk Modeling, 12 units
- Degree-specific core, 12 units: one more graduate security course, from a pre-approved list on the site
- 14-601 Academic & Professional Development, 3 units

MSIS students must take 14-513 Introduction to Computer Systems. It is the prerequisite for every systems core course, and the site recommends taking it in your first semester.

Electives can come from INI, ECE, SCS, EPP, ETC and III; Heinz and Tepper courses count only if they are on the approved list. MSIN's Standard option is also 75 core plus 48 elective units, but MSIN's core has only 12 units of security and 24 units of systems, and its business course is 14-776. In MSIS, security takes 36 core units and the business course is replaced by risk modeling.

If you want to specialize further, there is the Cyber Defense Specialization (open to MSIS and MSAIE-IS students), Cyber Operations (MSIS students earn it within the degree coursework, with no extra classes), and the CyFIR forensics and incident response track, which is open to all CMU students and comes with a certificate from the CERT Division of CMU's Software Engineering Institute.

## Internships / CPT / Co-op

Standard and Advanced do not require an internship, and INI does not arrange internships. If you want to intern, you can count it as an elective: register for a 3-unit internship course (Pass/No Pass) and pay additional tuition. According to the INI curriculum page, international students who start in fall may be eligible for pre-completion OPT in their first summer; spring starters on Standard are not eligible in their first summer, and spring starters on Advanced may be eligible in their second summer. The Applied page also says OIE currently advises international students who want to intern to apply for pre-completion OPT, but the INI internship page and the admissions FAQ still say an off-campus internship requires CPT eligibility. The pages do not agree, so go by what OIE tells you once you enroll.

From Fall 2027, students on Applied or Applied Advanced must complete a summer internship after their first full academic year to graduate. You find the internship yourself and INI does not guarantee one; the internship must be approved by INI before you accept an offer; and you register for the 3-unit INI internship course that summer, billed at the summer per-unit rate.

## Jobs and Location

From INI's outcomes page: the class of 2025 MSIS median first-destination salary was $145,000. Listed employers include Dell Technologies, Google, Praetorian and Sandia National Laboratories, and the listed locations were San Francisco, Seattle and Pittsburgh. For 2026 summer internships, the median hourly pay was $46, the listed employer was Sandia National Laboratories, and the top internship location was California.

## Application Requirements

For the Fall 2027 cycle:

- Deadlines: priority deadline December 1, 2026, final deadline January 29, 2027, both at 11:59 p.m. ET. The site notes the dates are tentative.
- Application fee: $75, waived if you register for and attend an INI info session.
- GRE: not required, and not reviewed if submitted.
- English: TOEFL, IELTS and Duolingo are accepted, with no published minimum. The FAQ gives a reference point: the strongest candidates usually have TOEFL section scores of 25 or higher, an IELTS overall band of 7.5 or higher, or a Duolingo score above 120, and lower scores do not mean rejection. Four years of in-residence undergraduate study in the U.S. waives the requirement.
- Recommendations: three letters. INI prefers one from a professional experience and two from faculty at your most recent school, but this is not required.
- Essays: three required and one optional, each up to 300 words, plus a three-minute video answering a random, non-technical prompt, with 60 seconds to prepare.
- Other: a resume of up to two pages, unofficial transcripts, and your programming experience and languages in the application. The minimum recommended qualifications are a bachelor's degree and a 3.0/4.0 GPA.
- One application lets you list a first-choice and a second-choice INI program.

## Comparison with Similar Programs

- [CMU MSIN](../S/cmu%20msin.md): also an INI program. Its Standard option is also 75 core plus 48 elective units, but the core leans toward systems and networking, with only 12 units of security.
- [CMU MSMITE](../A/CMU%20MSMITE.md): INI's mobile and IoT program, one of the Bicoastal programs, with study in both Pittsburgh and Silicon Valley.
- [CMU MSAIE](../A/CMU%20MSAIE.md): its MSAIE-IS version is another INI security degree with AI coursework added, and the Cyber Defense Specialization is open to it as well.
- [CMU MITS](../A/cmu%20mits.md): one of its four tracks is Information Security.
- [JHU MSSI](../B+/JHU%20MSSI.md): another information security master's program.
